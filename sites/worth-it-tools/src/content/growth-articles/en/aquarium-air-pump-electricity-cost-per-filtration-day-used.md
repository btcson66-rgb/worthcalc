# Aquarium Air Pump Electricity Cost Per Filtration Day Used: Include Back Pressure

> **Quick answer:** Define a filtration day by the pump schedule actually used, measure the pump path with its air stone and tubing, and divide by completed filtration days. Do not treat tank volume as a substitute for operating time. This is a cost estimate, not a saving, comfort, health, safety, performance, or income guarantee.

## Inputs that change the answer

Record pump speed, number of outlets, air stone, tubing length, water depth, timer, hours on, hours off, measured kWh, tariff, and date. Keep heater, light, filter motor, and other aquarium equipment outside this pump-only boundary. Test one outlet and the normal multi-outlet setup if both are used. Back pressure from depth, blocked stones, and narrow tubing can change the pump load, so document the exact arrangement rather than borrowing a wattage from another tank.

## Formula and measured example

electricity cost per completed filtration day = measured kWh for an aquarium air pump × delivered electricity rate ÷ completed units. If the pump boundary uses 0.06 kWh in a 24-hour schedule at 0.20 per kWh, the charge is 0.012 per filtration day. A timer that runs 12 hours creates a different day definition and should not be averaged silently. Rated power or product labels can help plan a test; a reliable answer keeps wall-meter start and end times, kWh, tariff, and date.

## Limits and common mistakes

Water depth, air resistance, stone condition, tubing, pump age, vibration, timer, ambient temperature, and shared outlets affect readings. Electricity cost is not a claim about animal health, oxygen level, or aquarium care. Run at least three repeatable measurements, keep the device boundary and tariff fixed, and preserve raw kWh plus the operating definition when comparing modes.

## Frequently asked question

### Can I divide by aquarium gallons or litres?

Not for a time-based cost. Use completed filtration days; tank volume can be a separate comparison field.
