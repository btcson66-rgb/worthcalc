# Bathroom Ventilation Fan Electricity Cost Per Shower Used: Include Run-On Timer

> **Quick answer:** Define one shower job as fan operation from the start of the shower through the normal run-on period. Measure the fan, light, and sensor load, then divide by completed showers. This is a cost estimate, not a saving, comfort, health, safety, performance, or income guarantee.

## Inputs that change the answer

Record airflow setting, light state, humidity sensor, start delay, run-on minutes, measured kWh, tariff, and date. Do not use shower length alone if the fan continues running after the water stops. Test the fan with the light off, the light on, and the automatic humidity or timer mode you normally use. ENERGY STAR ventilation-fan guidance distinguishes fan performance and airflow conditions; it does not give your bathroom’s measured energy.

## Formula and measured example

electricity cost per completed shower = measured kWh for a bathroom ventilation fan × delivered electricity rate ÷ completed units. A 25-minute fan boundary using 0.015 kWh at 0.20 per kWh costs 0.003 per shower. If the light adds 0.01 kWh, publish that combined boundary instead of treating it as fan-only. Rated power or product labels can help plan a test; a reliable answer keeps wall-meter start and end times, kWh, tariff, and date.

## Limits and common mistakes

Run-on timer, humidity control, airflow resistance, duct length, backdraft damper, light kit, and fan speed change readings. Electricity cost is not proof of moisture removal, air quality, noise, or installation compliance. Run at least three repeatable measurements, keep the device boundary and tariff fixed, and preserve raw kWh plus the operating definition when comparing modes.

## Frequently asked question

### Do I stop measuring when the shower ends?

No, not if the fan normally runs on. End the job at the normal timer or sensor stop and state that boundary.
