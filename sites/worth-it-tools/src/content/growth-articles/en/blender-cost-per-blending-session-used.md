# Blender Cost Per Blending Session Used

> **Quick answer:** Blender Cost Per Blending Session Used: estimate electricity from the measured routine, actual minutes, batch size, and delivered rate instead of treating the nameplate wattage as a universal cycle cost.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure pulse time, continuous blending, speed changes, ice or thick-food load, and whether washing is powered by a separate appliance.

## Formula and worked example

cost = measured kWh × delivered rate. Measure pulse time, continuous blending, speed changes, ice or thick-food load, and whether washing is powered by a separate appliance.
 A 600 W blender running 90 seconds in pulses uses about 0.015 kWh; at $0.20/kWh that is $0.003, but a long thick-food session should be measured rather than assumed. Divide by the completed cycles or sessions only when that is the question.

## Limits and common mistakes

Measure pulse time, continuous blending, speed changes, ice or thick-food load, and whether washing is powered by a separate appliance. Actual energy changes with voltage, thermostat cycling, load, pauses, standby, water or ingredients, and the tariff on the bill. Keep ingredients, water, labour, equipment depreciation, and cleanup separate; do not turn an estimate into a product-efficiency or health guarantee.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I use label watts or a wall measurement?

Measure pulse time, continuous blending, speed changes, ice or thick-food load, and whether washing is powered by a separate appliance. A 600 W blender running 90 seconds in pulses uses about 0.015 kWh; at $0.20/kWh that is $0.003, but a long thick-food session should be measured rather than assumed. When preheat, warming, pulses, or thermostat cycling exist, keep the kWh for the full routine instead of multiplying the rated watts alone.
