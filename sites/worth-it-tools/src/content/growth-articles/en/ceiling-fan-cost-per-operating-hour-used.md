# Ceiling Fan Cost Per Operating Hour Used: Separate Airflow From Lighting

> **Quick answer:** Estimate a ceiling fan's cost per operating hour from measured watts, speed, light kit use, electricity rates, controls, and equipment allocation instead of assuming every fan hour costs the same.

## Inputs that change the result

Record fan watts at each speed, light-kit watts, hours, number of fans, electricity rate, delivery charges, remote or control standby if material, purchase price, service, and useful life.

## Formula and worked example

effective fan-hour cost = (fan energy + light energy + delivery charges + maintenance + equipment allocation) ÷ fan operating hours. A 55-watt fan for 8 hours uses 0.44 kWh. At $0.20 per kWh, energy is $0.088 before fixed charges, lighting, and equipment allocation. Report the fan and light kit separately when possible.

## Compare real use cases

Compare low, medium, and high speed and whether the light is on. ENERGY STAR expresses ceiling-fan efficiency as airflow per watt across speeds and standby; airflow comfort is not the same as cooling delivered by an air conditioner.

## Limits and common mistakes

Do not call a fan an air-conditioner substitute or promise a fixed bill reduction. Room size, ceiling height, speed, light use, controls, and the electricity rate change the result.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should the light kit be included?

Include it only when the question is total fixture cost. For a motor comparison, separate light-kit energy so the fan efficiency is not hidden by unrelated lighting use.
