# Ceiling Fan Electricity Cost Per Cooling Hour Used: Separate Light and Standby

> **Quick answer:** Measure the fan motor, any light kit, remote receiver, and standby window at the speed you actually use. Divide the measured electricity charge by completed cooling hours, not by the number of hot days on the calendar. This is a cost estimate, not a saving, comfort, health, safety, performance, or income guarantee.

## Inputs that change the answer

Record blade size, speed, light state, remote receiver, hours running, hours in standby, measured kWh, tariff, and date. If the fan has a light kit, publish fan-only and fan-plus-light results rather than hiding the lamp load. Run low, medium, and high-speed tests with the light off, then repeat with the light on if it is part of normal use. Ceiling-fan efficiency is reported with operating modes and standby context, but a product page cannot provide the reading for your ceiling height, controller, or tariff.

## Formula and measured example

electricity cost per completed cooling hour = measured kWh for a ceiling fan × delivered electricity rate ÷ completed units. If the defined fan boundary uses 0.12 kWh during 4 cooling hours at 0.20 per kWh, the charge is 0.024, or 0.006 per cooling hour. A light-kit charge should be listed separately when it changes the question. Rated power or product labels can help plan a test; a reliable answer keeps wall-meter start and end times, kWh, tariff, and date.

## Limits and common mistakes

Blade size, ceiling height, speed control, light kit, remote receiver, room temperature, wiring, and standby time change the reading. Electricity cost does not prove room temperature, comfort, installation quality, or energy savings for every home. Run at least three repeatable measurements, keep the device boundary and tariff fixed, and preserve raw kWh plus the operating definition when comparing modes.

## Frequently asked question

### Should a ceiling-fan light be included?

Include it only for a fan-plus-light boundary. For fan-only cost, isolate the motor path and report the light separately.
