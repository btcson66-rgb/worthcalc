# Childcare Cost Per Hour Used: Price the Schedule You Actually Need

> **Quick answer:** Compare childcare by the hours you actually purchase and use, including registration, transport, late pickup, and backup costs—not just the advertised weekly rate.

## Collect the inputs that change the result

Record the scheduled hours, hours truly used, weekly fee, registration, meals, transport, late charges, backup care, and refundable deposits. Separate work-required hours from optional convenience hours.

## Formula and worked example

effective cost per used hour = (care fee + required extras + backup care + transport) ÷ hours actually used. A $1,200 monthly plan includes 160 scheduled hours, but a family uses 120 and pays $60 in registration and $40 in transport. Effective cost is $10.83 per used hour, not $7.50 per scheduled hour.

## Use a range instead of a promise

Run a normal month, school-break month, and late-pickup month. Compare the hours, reliability, commute, and cash timing of each option rather than treating care as a single sticker price. Run low-use, realistic-use, and high-use cases. If the result changes, show the range and the assumption that changes it. Do not count hours that are available but never needed. Also check whether a lower hourly result creates unpaid travel time or a backup-care gap.

Keep a short use log for a month or project. Separate unavoidable costs from optional extras, record missed uses, and check the current terms before the next charge. This makes the denominator auditable instead of optimistic.
