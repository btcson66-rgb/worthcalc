# Cloud Storage Cost Per Retained GB: Include Duplicate and Unused Capacity

> **Quick answer:** Measure cloud storage by the data you keep and need, not by the maximum capacity on the plan; include taxes, add-ons, migration, and renewal price.

## Collect the inputs that change the result

Record monthly or annual price, taxes, family or team add-ons, storage actually retained, duplicate files, migration time, download fees, and the next renewal price.

## Formula and worked example

effective cost per retained GB = (subscription + taxes + add-ons + required transfer cost) ÷ retained GB that you actively need. A $12 plan plus $2 tax stores 140 GB, but only 80 GB is needed after removing duplicates. The effective cost is $0.175 per needed GB, not $0.10 per advertised GB.

## Use a range instead of a promise

Run current-use, growth, and cleanup scenarios. Compare deleting duplicates, local storage, and a lower tier while checking recovery and sharing requirements. Run low-use, realistic-use, and high-use cases. If the result changes, show the range and the assumption that changes it. Do not count promotional capacity or duplicate files as useful value. A lower price is not a saving if downgrading removes needed recovery or access.

Keep a short use log for a month or cycle. Separate unavoidable costs from optional extras, record missed uses, and check the current terms before the next charge. This makes the denominator auditable instead of optimistic.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can everyone use the same threshold?

No. Prices, usage, timing, and terms differ. Replace the example with your records and keep low, base, and high cases visible.

### What should I check first?

How do I compare cloud storage plans? Divide complete current charges by the retained capacity you genuinely need, then test renewal and recovery terms. Recalculate when terms, price, or usage change.
