# Coffee Maker Cost Per Brew Used: Separate Brewing, Warming, and the Cup Served

> **Quick answer:** Estimate a coffee maker electricity cost per brew from measured wall energy for heating and warming, the coffee volume actually served, and your delivered electricity rate instead of treating the nameplate wattage as a full cycle.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure wall kWh from the start of a brew through the end of the warming period you actually use. Record water volume, brew time, warming minutes, automatic shutoff, grinder or milk heater, number of cups served, rate, and coffee that is discarded.

## Formula and worked example

cost per brew = measured wall kWh for brewing and chosen warming period × delivered electricity rate. Cost per cup = brew electricity plus attributable equipment energy divided by cups actually consumed; coffee beans, filters, and milk are separate inputs. A drip machine uses 0.115 kWh to brew 0.75 litres and 0.025 kWh while warming for 20 minutes, or 0.14 kWh total. At $0.20/kWh, direct electricity is $0.028 per batch. If only two of four cups are consumed, cost per consumed cup is higher than the machine-only batch figure.

## Compare real use cases

Compare a single-cup pod machine, a full drip batch consumed promptly, and a pot kept warm then partly discarded. A grinder, hot plate, and automatic rinse can shift the wall reading; state whether the result is per brew, per served cup, or per consumed cup.

## Limits and common mistakes

Water temperature, batch size, warming plate, thermal carafe, grinder, descaling, automatic cycles, voltage, and wasted coffee change energy. Do not mix electricity with beans or subscription costs, and do not call one machine’s measured cycle a universal coffee price.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I count the warming plate?

Count it when the question is the real brew routine. Measure or allocate the warming period, then show brew-only and brew-plus-warming results separately so a long keep-warm habit is visible.
