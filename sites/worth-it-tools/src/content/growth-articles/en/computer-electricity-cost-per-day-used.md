# Computer Electricity Cost Per Day Used: Separate Work, Sleep, and Charging

> **Quick answer:** Estimate a computer's electricity cost per day from measured watts in work, idle, sleep, and charging modes, plus the local delivered-electricity rate; a laptop's adapter rating is not its daily bill.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure wall watts or interval kWh for work, idle, sleep, and charging. Record hours in each mode, external monitor power, battery charging, rate-plan and delivery charges, and whether the device serves one person or a shared household task.

## Formula and worked example

daily computer energy cost = (work kWh + idle kWh + sleep kWh + charging kWh) × delivered electricity rate + attributable equipment allocation. Keep the computer separate from the monitor and dock so a desktop setup is not understated. A laptop averages 35 W for 7 work hours, 8 W for 2 idle hours, and 1 W for 15 sleep hours, using 0.263 kWh. At $0.20/kWh, direct energy is about $0.05 per day. A desktop, display, dock, and overnight charging can be materially different, so use measured modes rather than the charger maximum.

## Compare real use cases

Compare a laptop that sleeps between meetings, a desktop left idle during a full workday, and a device that remains connected overnight for updates. ENERGY STAR says certified computers address multiple operating modes and power management; that programme context does not predict your model, workload, or tariff.

## Limits and common mistakes

Processor load, screen brightness, peripherals, charging losses, updates, external displays, and sleep settings change consumption. Do not multiply the power-supply label by 24 hours unless the computer truly draws that power continuously, and do not call a lower bill guaranteed from a label alone.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should a laptop and charger be counted together?

For a household bill, measure the wall input of the complete setup, including the charger. For a device comparison, report the computer and charger method consistently, and do not compare a battery-only reading with an AC-wall reading.
