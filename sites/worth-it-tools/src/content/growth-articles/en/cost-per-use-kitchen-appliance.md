# Kitchen Appliance Cost Per Use: Include Energy, Cleaning, and Replacement

> **Quick answer:** Appliance cost per use includes purchase, energy or water, consumables, cleaning, repairs, and the realistic number of completed uses.

## Collect the inputs that change the result

Enter purchase price, installation, energy or water per cycle, consumables, cleaning time or cost, repairs, useful life, and expected cycles. Use measured or label-based energy estimates and keep local rates as inputs.

## Formula and worked example

cost per use = (purchase + installation + lifetime operating cost + repairs − realistic resale) ÷ completed uses. A $360 appliance costs $40 to install and $0.45 per cycle. Over 400 cycles with $50 maintenance, total is $630, or $1.58 per completed use. If only 150 cycles occur, it is $4.20.

## Use a range instead of a promise

ENERGY STAR consumer material describes an appliance as having both a purchase price and ongoing energy or water costs. Compare those layers with the smaller appliance or existing manual method. Run low-use, realistic-use, and high-use cases. If the result changes, show the range and the assumption that changes it. Do not treat a technical maximum cycle count as your own usage. A cheaper energy label cannot recover a purchase that sits unused.

Keep a short usage log for a month or cycle. Separate unavoidable costs from optional add-ons, and record why a planned use did not happen. That record makes the denominator auditable instead of optimistic.
