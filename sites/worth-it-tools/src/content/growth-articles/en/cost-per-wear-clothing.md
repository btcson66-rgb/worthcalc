# Cost Per Wear Calculator: Price Clothing by Realistic Uses

> **Quick answer:** Cost per wear is the purchase price, alterations, care, and repair divided by the number of wears you realistically expect—not the number you hope for.

## Collect the inputs that change the result

Record the purchase price, tailoring, cleaning, repair, storage, expected wears, and resale value only if resale is realistic. Compare the item with a garment you already own or a lower-cost alternative that meets the same need.

## Formula and worked example

cost per wear = (price + care + repair + alterations − realistic resale value) ÷ expected wears. A $120 jacket needs $20 tailoring and $30 cleaning over its useful life. At 25 wears, cost per wear is $6.80. At 60 wears it is $2.83. The difference is usage, not a magic quality score.

## Use a range instead of a promise

This is a use-based value question. Test low, normal, and high wear counts, and include weather, fit, laundry rules, and whether the item works with clothes already in the wardrobe. Run low-use, realistic-use, and high-use cases. If the result changes, show the range and the assumption that changes it. Do not use resale value or “cost per wear” to justify buying something you will not wear. A lower per-use result does not make an unwanted purchase necessary.

Keep a short usage log for a month or cycle. Separate unavoidable costs from optional add-ons, and record why a planned use did not happen. That record makes the denominator auditable instead of optimistic.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can everyone use the same threshold?

No. Prices, usage, timing, and terms differ. Replace the example with your records and keep low, base, and high cases visible.

### What should I check first?

What is a good cost per wear? There is no universal threshold. Use your own alternative, expected wears, and care cost; compare like-for-like needs. Recalculate when terms, price, or usage change.
