# Costco Gas Detour Break-Even: Is the Lower Price Worth the Extra Drive?

> **Quick answer:** A gas detour is worthwhile only when the fuel-price savings on the amount you actually buy exceed the extra driving cost and any membership cost allocated to the trip. A lower posted price can still lose money when the detour is long, the tank is nearly full, or the queue causes a separate trip.

## Measure the detour, not the headline price

Record the price difference per gallon, the gallons you would actually buy, extra miles, vehicle fuel economy, and your marginal fuel price. Include tolls, parking, and a separate membership allocation if the trip exists only for the warehouse. Do not allocate the entire annual membership to every fuel purchase; choose a transparent rule, such as dividing the fee across the number of planned qualifying trips, and show the sensitivity.

The official [Costco Executive Rewards information](https://www.costco.com/f/-/executive-rewards) also lists fuel among categories that may be excluded from the Executive 2% reward in its U.S. terms. That means a lower pump price and a reward calculation are separate questions. Check your local terms instead of adding a reward automatically.

## Formula and worked example

Extra driving cost equals extra miles divided by vehicle miles per gallon, multiplied by the marginal fuel price, plus tolls and parking. Fuel saving equals price gap per gallon multiplied by gallons purchased. Net trip value equals fuel saving minus extra driving cost minus the allocated membership cost.

Suppose the price gap is $0.18 per gallon and you buy 12 gallons, for $2.16 of gross saving. The detour adds 8 miles in a vehicle that gets 24 mpg; at $3.60 per gallon, the extra fuel costs 8 ÷ 24 × $3.60 = $1.20. Add $1.50 of allocated trip or membership cost and the net result is negative $0.54. At 20 gallons, gross saving is $3.60 and the same trip produces $0.90 net value, but only if the larger purchase is useful and safe to store.

Run a no-detour comparison. If you were already visiting Costco for planned groceries, count only the incremental distance caused by choosing its fuel station. If the fuel stop creates the whole trip, use the full route. Traffic, waiting, cold starts, and a route that displaces another errand can move the result.

## Use a range and avoid double counting

Fuel economy from a dashboard estimate may differ from the route’s real result. Use a low and high consumption case. Keep the household vehicle’s normal fuel cost separate from the incremental detour cost. Also keep membership reward exclusions separate from pump-price savings; the same dollar should not be credited twice.

The [membership fee break-even guide](/en/guides/costco-membership-fee-break-even-savings/) handles the wider account decision. The [reward exclusion guide](/en/guides/costco-reward-exclusion-effective-rate/) helps classify purchases. If fuel is one of several reasons for membership, add each reason only when it represents an otherwise-planned use.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does filling the tank make the detour worthwhile?

Not automatically. More gallons increase the possible saving, but an unnecessary fill is extra spending and may violate your normal storage or travel plan.

### Should I value my time?

Keep time as a separate scenario. You can use a personal time value for a decision, but do not mix it into the posted-price discount and call the result an objective fuel saving.

### Is the Executive reward included?

Only if your current official terms say that the fuel purchase qualifies. The Costco pages list exclusions, so verify before modeling it.

Review your actual routes and receipts using the [CFPB spending assessment](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/prepare/assess-your-spending/). This article estimates a trip; it does not recommend a membership or fuel purchase.
