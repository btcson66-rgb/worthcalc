# Countertop Ice Maker Cost Per Batch Used: Include Freeze and Hold Time

> **Quick answer:** Estimate countertop ice maker electricity cost per batch from measured freeze, harvest, and hold energy, then separate electricity from water, cleaning, and output weight. This is a cost estimate, not a guarantee of savings, product performance, food quality, health, or safety.

## Inputs that change the result

For this countertop ice maker, define the electricity boundary before looking at a per-batch number. Record water pumping, freezing, ice harvest, and any storage or keep-cold period from a repeatable start to a repeatable end. Keep starting temperature, load, mode, finished output, warm-hold behavior, tariff unit, and date together. A recipe duration, package instruction, or nameplate wattage is not a wall-meter kWh reading.

Use a plug-in meter for at least three repeatable runs and preserve the readings and tariff source. A bill can combine generation, delivery, taxes, fees, and fixed charges; an appliance comparison should state whether it uses a marginal kWh rate and should list fixed charges separately.

## Formula and worked example

Cost per run = measured wall kWh from the stated start to the stated end × delivered electricity rate. Cost per batch = that electricity cost ÷ the number of finished batchs. A measured 0.65 kWh run at $0.18/kWh costs $0.117 in electricity. If the run produces 1.5 kg of ice, the electricity allocation is $0.078 per kg. Water, filters, cleaning solution, and melt loss remain separate. This demonstrates allocation; it is not a forecast for a particular model, tariff, or food output.

Do not multiply the maximum rated watts by the entire recipe or holding time unless the meter confirms continuous operation at that load. Thermostat cycling, motor load, adapter losses, ventilation, holding behavior, and automatic shutoff can all change the reading. For a very short load, group several normal runs and divide the combined kWh.

## Compare real usage scenarios

Measure a warm restart, a cold restart, and a run where ice sits in the basket. Ambient temperature, water temperature, ventilation clearance, ice-size setting, and melt protection can change both cycle length and average draw. A per-batch answer should name the batch endpoint. Change one condition at a time and keep the denominator explicit: per run, per batch, or per kilogram, cup, or serving. If output weight, portions, or success rate changes, put it beside the result; a lower number can simply reflect a smaller load.

## Limits and common mistakes

Voltage, tariff design, room temperature, food, container, cleanliness, consumables, wear, and meter resolution affect the result. For full ownership cost, list purchase price, ingredients, water, cleaning, maintenance, refrigeration, waste, and time separately instead of hiding them in electricity. This is a cost estimate, not a guarantee of savings, product performance, food quality, health, or safety.

## Frequently asked question

### Should stored ice count as part of the batch?

Count it only through a stated endpoint. If the machine continues cooling or cycles after harvest, either include that measured energy or report it as a separate holding scenario.
