# Coworking Membership Cost Per Day: Include Access You Do Not Use

> **Quick answer:** Divide the coworking fee, setup, commute, locker, and meeting-room charges by days you actually work there—not open days in the calendar.

## Collect the inputs that change the result

Enter membership, joining, locker, meeting-room, coffee, commute, and cancellation costs. Count actual attendance and compare with home working or pay-as-you-go access.

## Formula and worked example

effective cost per attended day = (membership + extras + commute + switching cost) ÷ attended days. A $240 monthly plan has $30 locker and $48 commuting cost. At 12 visits, cost is $26.50 per day; at 4 visits, it is $79.50. The plan did not change—the use did.

## Use a range instead of a promise

Separate workspace value from productivity claims. Count days you would otherwise need a desk, and keep uncertain networking or motivation as a separate scenario. Run low-use, realistic-use, and high-use cases. If the result changes, show the range and the assumption that changes it. Do not call every open day a benefit. If holidays, remote days, or commute friction reduce attendance, include them in the low-use case.

Keep a short usage log for a month or cycle. Separate unavoidable costs from optional add-ons, and record why a planned use did not happen. That record makes the denominator auditable instead of optimistic.

### Can everyone use the same threshold?

No. Prices, usage, timing, and terms differ. Replace the example with your records and keep low, base, and high cases visible.

### What should I check first?

How many days make coworking worthwhile? Compare your attended-day cost with a realistic alternative and the cash-flow impact; there is no universal number. Recalculate when terms, price, or usage change.
