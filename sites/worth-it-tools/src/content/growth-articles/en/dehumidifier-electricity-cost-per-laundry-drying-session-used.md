# Dehumidifier Electricity Cost Per Drying Hour Used: Include Compressor Pauses

> **Quick answer:** Estimate dehumidifier cost per completed drying hour by including compressor, fan, defrost, and drain-pump states.

## Inputs that change the result

A dehumidifier can run its compressor and fan in cycles, pause at a humidity target, defrost, or operate a drain pump. Define a completed drying hour before reading the meter and state whether the result describes active drying time, clock time, or a room humidity window. Record the device state, start and stop readings, measured kWh, delivered rate, and exact window. Define one completed unit before calculating, and list any shared outlet load rather than assuming it is zero.

## Formula and measured example

Cost per completed drying hour = measured kWh × delivered electricity rate ÷ completed drying hours. If the meter records 0.7 kWh at 0.21 per kWh across 2 completed drying hours, the illustrative result is 0.0735 per hour. It is not a universal appliance average. Keep the raw readings, currency, date, and rate beside the result so another person can reproduce it. A rounded value without its measurement boundary is not an auditable household cost.

## Measurement method

Record starting and ending relative humidity, temperature, target, compressor state, fan setting, defrost events, water collection or drain mode, measured kWh, rate, and window. Use repeatable windows but retain humidity and temperature so a short easy run is not mistaken for a general result. If the load is estimated rather than measured, label the assumption and show how it changes the result. Repeat comparable windows, but do not erase a real operating difference just to make the numbers look tidy.

## Limits and common mistakes

Drying speed, mold prevention, comfort, noise, and air quality are separate claims and should not be inferred from electricity cost. Do not use rated watts as a bill, and do not compare rooms with different moisture sources without reporting that difference. Keep electricity separate from maintenance, water, consumables, repair, and any claimed outcome. At least three comparable readings can reveal variation, but repetition does not turn a small sample into a universal product claim.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a longer run always mean a higher cost per drying hour?

Not necessarily. Compressor cycling and humidity conditions can change the average. Use measured kWh and the same completed-hour definition rather than assuming every clock hour has the same load.
