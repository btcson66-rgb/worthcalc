# Electric Griddle Cost Per Cooking Hour Used: Separate Heat-Up and Active Cooking

> **Quick answer:** Work out an electric griddle electricity cost per cooking hour from measured heat-up, thermostat cycling, active plate time, and the area actually used instead of treating the rated wattage as constant draw.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure from a cold plug-in to the end of the cooking session. Record surface temperature setting, preheat minutes, active cooking minutes, food batches, thermostat off-cycles, warm-hold time, and whether the whole plate or only a section is used.

## Formula and worked example

cost per cooking hour = measured wall kWh for the defined session ÷ active cooking hours × delivered electricity rate. For cost per batch, multiply the session kWh by the rate and do not substitute plate area for time. A griddle uses 0.16 kWh to preheat and 0.24 kWh while cooking three batches over 45 active minutes: 0.40 kWh. At $0.20/kWh, the session is $0.080, or $0.0267 per batch before ingredients and washing.

## Compare real use cases

Compare a single breakfast, a family batch that keeps the plate warm, and short bursts on a lower setting. Thermostat cycling and batch count can matter more than the label wattage, especially when preheat is shared.

## Limits and common mistakes

Surface setting, room temperature, food moisture, pan contact, thermostat duty cycle, unused plate area, ventilation, voltage, pauses, and cleaning alter energy. Do not claim a griddle is cheaper than another cooking method without matching food, portions, time, and measured tariff.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should unused griddle surface be allocated to the food?

Allocate energy by the question you are answering. For appliance cost, count the whole measured session; for a recipe comparison, report batches and portions and explain that unused heated area is overhead.
