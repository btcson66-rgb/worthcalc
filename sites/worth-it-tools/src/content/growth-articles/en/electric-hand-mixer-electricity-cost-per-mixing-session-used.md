# Electric Hand Mixer Electricity Cost Per Mixing Session Used: Count Speed

> **Quick answer:** Define a mixing session as one completed recipe step or batch. Measure motor runtime, speed changes, and any plugged-in idle time inside that batch, then divide the charge by completed sessions. This is a cost estimate, not a guarantee of quality, health, safety, comfort, performance, or income.

## Inputs that change the answer

Record beaters, speed, motor minutes, pauses, plugged-in time, measured kWh, tariff, and date. Keep ingredients, washing, and a separate kitchen light outside the electricity boundary unless the question asks for total batch cost.

## Formula and measured example

A measured mixer boundary of 0.025 kWh at 0.20 per kWh costs 0.005. If it completes one mixing session, that is 0.005 per session; if two batches share the run, state the allocation rule. Formula: cost per completed unit = measured kWh × delivered electricity rate ÷ completed units.

Compare low-speed batter, high-speed cream, and a stop-start recipe. Use completed batches as the denominator only when each batch receives the measured work. ## Limits and common mistakes

Mixture thickness, speed, beater load, motor temperature, pauses, voltage, and washing choices affect readings. Electricity cost does not prove texture, food safety, or recipe success. Run at least three repeatable measurements, keep the device boundary and tariff fixed, and preserve raw kWh plus the operating definition when comparing modes.

## Frequently asked question

### Can I divide one run across two batches?

Yes, if both batches receive comparable measured work. State the allocation rule and keep ingredients and washing as separate costs.
