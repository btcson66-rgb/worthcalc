# Electric Kettle Cost Per Boil Used: Account for Water Volume and Shutoff

> **Quick answer:** Estimate an electric kettle cost per boil from measured wall energy, water volume, starting temperature, keep-warm behavior, and your delivered electricity rate rather than the kettle’s maximum wattage.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure wall kWh for the volume you normally boil, noting starting water temperature, fill level, lid position, altitude if relevant, automatic shutoff, and any keep-warm period. Record whether one boil serves one drink or a shared pot.

## Formula and worked example

cost per boil = measured wall kWh for the selected water volume and temperature rise × delivered electricity rate. For cost per cup, divide by the actual cups served, not the kettle capacity or the number printed on the package. A kettle uses 0.105 kWh to heat 0.75 litres from room temperature to boiling. At $0.20/kWh, direct energy is about $0.021 per boil, or $0.005 per 250 ml cup if all four cups are used. Reboiling unused water changes the denominator and the cost.

## Compare real use cases

Compare one mug, a full kettle for several people, and a boil followed by 30 minutes of keep-warm. Electric kettles can have high instantaneous watts but short run time; a measured kWh session answers the bill question more reliably than multiplying the label by an hour.

## Limits and common mistakes

Water volume, starting temperature, mineral scale, lid, voltage, altitude, auto shutoff, keep-warm heating, and wasted water affect energy. Do not claim a universal cost per cup, and do not count tea, coffee, filters, or the value of waiting as electricity unless separated.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is boiling a full kettle always cheaper per cup?

Usually the comparison depends on how much water you actually use and whether every cup is consumed. Measure the same served volume for each method; unused hot water can make a full-kettle result look artificially efficient.
