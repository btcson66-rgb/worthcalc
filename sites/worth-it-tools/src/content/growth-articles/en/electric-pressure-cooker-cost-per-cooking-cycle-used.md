# Electric Pressure Cooker Cost Per Cooking Cycle Used

> **Quick answer:** Estimate an electric pressure cooker electricity cost per cooking cycle from measured wall energy for preheat, pressure cooking, release, and keep-warm time instead of multiplying the maximum wattage by the recipe duration.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure from a repeatable cold or room-temperature start until the meal is finished. Record pot fill, ingredients, pressure-build minutes, active cooking time, natural or quick release, keep-warm minutes, lid changes, and the number of portions actually served.

## Formula and worked example

cost per pressure-cooking cycle = measured wall kWh from start through the chosen end point × delivered electricity rate. Cost per portion = cycle electricity divided by portions served; ingredients, water, and the time spent preparing or cleaning are separate. A cooker uses 0.08 kWh to build pressure and 0.12 kWh during 35 minutes of cooking, release, and 20 minutes of keep-warm: 0.20 kWh for a six-portion pot. At $0.20/kWh, direct electricity is $0.040 per pot or about $0.0067 per portion before food and cleanup.

## Compare real use cases

Compare a small half-filled pot, a full batch that uses natural release, and a recipe that keeps warm for two hours. The same programme can have different energy per serving when batch size, release method, or keep-warm behaviour changes.

## Limits and common mistakes

Pot load, liquid volume, starting temperature, pressure programme, release method, lid sealing, keep-warm thermostat, voltage, and reheating change the wall reading. Do not treat pressure cooking as a guaranteed energy saving or make food-safety claims from a cost estimate.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should keep-warm time be included?

Include it when your household normally leaves the cooker warming until the meal is served. Show a cooking-only figure and a cooking-plus-keep-warm figure so the extra time is visible rather than hidden in a recipe comparison.
