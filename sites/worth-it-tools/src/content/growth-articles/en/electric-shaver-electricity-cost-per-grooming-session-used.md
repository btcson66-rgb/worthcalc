# Electric Shaver Electricity Cost Per Grooming Session Used: Count Charging

> **Quick answer:** Define one grooming session before measuring: motor runtime, cleaning cycle, and the charging window that belongs to the shaver. Divide measured kWh by completed sessions, not by advertised battery life. This is a cost estimate, not a guarantee of quality, health, safety, comfort, performance, or income.

## Inputs that change the answer

Record foil or rotary mode, minutes of motor use, cleaning-station cycles, charging time, measured kWh, tariff, and date. Report the shaver alone and the shaver-plus-cleaning-station boundary separately.

## Formula and measured example

A 0.018 kWh measured boundary at 0.20 per kWh costs 0.0036. If that run completes two grooming sessions, the allocated cost is 0.0018 per session. The session definition controls the result. Formula: cost per completed unit = measured kWh × delivered electricity rate ÷ completed units.

## Compare real operating cases

Compare a dry shave, a longer trim, and a cleaning-station cycle after use. Do not distribute a weekly cleaning cycle across sessions unless the allocation rule is stated. ## Limits and common mistakes

Hair density, pressure, wet or dry mode, cleaning fluid, station heating, battery state, and charging habits affect readings. Cost does not prove closeness, skin comfort, hygiene, or blade life. Run at least three repeatable measurements, keep the device boundary and tariff fixed, and preserve raw kWh plus the operating definition when comparing modes.

## Frequently asked question

### How should a cleaning station be allocated?

Measure the station as its own boundary, then assign its cycle to the sessions it actually serves. Keep the rule visible instead of hiding it in a daily average.
