# Electric Standing Desk Cost Per Adjusted Day Used: Include Motor and Standby

> **Quick answer:** Estimate electric standing desk electricity cost per adjusted day from measured motor movements, controller, and standby energy, separating monitor and computer load. This is a cost estimate, not a savings, product-performance, ranking, or household-outcome guarantee.

## What changes the result

electric standing desk cost per adjusted day starts with a declared wall-meter boundary. Start and end with a declared wall-meter boundary. Record the device state, load, connected accessories, completed unit, tariff unit, and measurement date. A rating or typical value can plan a test but cannot replace a repeatable reading.

The desk motor may run for seconds while the control box remains connected for the rest of the day. Define an adjusted day as the period you want to compare, and do not turn a desk-only reading into a whole workstation estimate.

## Formula and worked example

Electricity cost = measured wall kWh × delivered electricity rate. Unit cost = electricity cost ÷ completed units. Keep fixed fees, taxes, supplies, repairs, subscriptions, and the computer or network load outside the numerator unless your stated boundary intentionally includes them. A desk-only reading of 0.018 kWh at $0.18/kWh costs $0.00324 for the adjusted day. Add a separately measured monitor or computer only if the question is the complete desk setup.

Do not multiply a rated wattage by the whole elapsed period unless the wall meter confirms that load. If a computer, display, network device, charger, or accessory is present, measure it separately before combining boundaries.

## Compare real use cases

Compare no movement, a normal morning adjustment, and frequent changes. Record load on the desktop, number of movements, anti-collision events, controller state, and the exact standby window.

## Limits and common mistakes

Voltage, tariff structure, firmware, room conditions, mode, load, measurement resolution, standby window, and failed work can change the result. Test at least three repeatable sessions, combine short runs when the meter resolution is coarse, and compare one changed condition at a time. The estimate is not a promise of savings, performance, or household results.
