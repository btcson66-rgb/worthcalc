# Electric Toothbrush Electricity Cost Per Charging Day Used: Include Dock Standby

> **Quick answer:** Measure the toothbrush, charging dock, and idle window that belongs to one charging day. Divide the measured charge by completed charging days, rather than treating the battery label as a household bill. This is a cost estimate, not a guarantee of quality, health, safety, comfort, performance, or income.

## Inputs that change the answer

Record brush mode, charging state, dock time, battery level before and after, measured kWh, tariff, and date. A dock can draw differently while charging and after the battery is full, so keep those windows separate when the question is charging cost.

## Formula and measured example

If one dock boundary uses 0.006 kWh during a measured 24-hour charging day at 0.20 per kWh, the charge is 0.0012 per charging day. This example is a calculation pattern, not a typical toothbrush result. Formula: cost per completed unit = measured kWh × delivered electricity rate ÷ completed units.

Compare a brush that returns to the dock after each use, a scheduled recharge, and a full-battery day away from the dock. ## Limits and common mistakes

Battery chemistry, dock electronics, adapter losses, brush pressure, cleaning cycles, and time left on the dock affect readings. Electricity cost does not establish brushing quality, battery health, hygiene, or safety. Run at least three repeatable measurements, keep the device boundary and tariff fixed, and preserve raw kWh plus the operating definition when comparing modes.

## Frequently asked question

### Should the charging dock stay in the calculation?

Include it when the question is the cost of the complete charging setup. For charger-only cost, isolate the adapter and report dock electronics separately.
