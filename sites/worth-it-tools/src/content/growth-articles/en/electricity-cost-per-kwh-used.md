# Electricity Cost Per kWh Used: Include Fixed Charges and Credits

> **Quick answer:** Measure an electricity bill by the kilowatt-hours actually used, while keeping fixed delivery charges, taxes, demand charges, and credits visible.

## Collect the inputs that change the result

Record the supply charge, delivery charge, monthly service fee, taxes, renewable or demand fees, solar export credits, total kWh used, billing days, and any late or reconnection charge. Use the bill period rather than a guessed monthly average.

## Formula and worked example

effective cost per kWh used = (supply + delivery + fixed charges + taxes + usage-related fees − applicable credits) ÷ kWh used. A bill has $42 for supply, $28 for delivery, $12 in fixed charges, and $3 tax, with a $5 credit and 500 kWh used. The effective cost is $80 ÷ 500 = $0.16 per used kWh. Dividing only the supply line would hide most of the cash cost.

## Use a range instead of a promise

Compare a low-use month, a typical month, and a high-use month. Fixed charges make the unit cost rise when usage falls, while a tiered rate or time-of-use plan can change the result even when total kWh is similar. If solar credits, a demand charge, or a shared meter is involved, keep each item as a separate scenario instead of forcing it into one advertised rate.

EIA distinguishes average retail prices from utility rates and notes that delivered electricity can include generation, transmission, distribution, taxes, and fees. Do not copy a national or state average as your personal rate. This calculation explains your recorded bill; it does not predict future weather, tariff changes, or savings from a device.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can every household use the same threshold?

No. Rate structure, fixed charges, timing, and credits vary; use your own bill to build low, base, and high cases.

### When should I recalculate?

How do I calculate my real electricity rate? Add the bill lines that were actually charged, subtract credits that actually reduced the bill, and divide by the kWh on that bill. Recalculate after a rate change or a different billing period.
