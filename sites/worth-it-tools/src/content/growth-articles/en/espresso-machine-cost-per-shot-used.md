# Espresso Machine Cost Per Shot Used: Include Heat-Up, Brew, and Steam

> **Quick answer:** Calculate an espresso machine electricity cost per shot from measured heat-up, brewing, steaming, and standby energy allocated across the shots actually served instead of using the boiler rating alone.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure a defined routine from a cold start or a settled idle state. Record warm-up minutes, boiler or thermoblock cycling, brew seconds, steam or milk heating, rinses, standby interval, shots pulled, and shots discarded during dialing in.

## Formula and worked example

cost per shot = measured wall kWh for the defined coffee routine × delivered electricity rate ÷ shots served. Report a cold-start single shot separately from a multi-shot session because setup energy is shared differently. An espresso machine uses 0.10 kWh to warm, 0.04 kWh to brew and steam four drinks, and 0.02 kWh in a short standby period: 0.16 kWh. At $0.20/kWh, electricity is $0.032 for the session or $0.008 per served drink before coffee, milk, water, and cleaning.

## Compare real use cases

Compare one shot after a cold start, four drinks made together, and a long idle period with a hot boiler. Dial-in shots and automatic rinses are part of the routine when they happen in actual use.

## Limits and common mistakes

Machine architecture, boiler size, temperature control, steam demand, water temperature, rinse cycles, standby, voltage, ambient temperature, grind adjustments, and discarded shots alter energy. Do not present electricity as the full price of espresso or as a taste, caffeine, or health claim.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I spread warm-up energy across every shot?

Spread one measured warm-up across shots served in the same continuous session. For a single occasional shot, keep the cold-start result visible; for a café-style batch, report the shared setup and any discarded calibration shots.
