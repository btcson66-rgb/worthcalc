# Hair Dryer Cost Per Drying Session Used: Separate Heat, Airflow, and Minutes

> **Quick answer:** Calculate a hair dryer electricity cost per drying session from measured watts at each heat and airflow setting, actual minutes, and your delivered electricity rate instead of multiplying the highest rating by an hour.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure wall watts on cool, low, and high heat and airflow settings. Record pre-drying towel time, powered minutes, pauses, diffuser or concentrator, household voltage, rate, and whether the session is one person or shared styling.

## Formula and worked example

drying-session electricity cost = sum of measured watts × minutes for each setting ÷ 60,000 × delivered electricity rate. If comparing routines, keep towel drying and powered drying separate and use the same hair result standard. A dryer draws 1,200 W for 8 minutes on high and 500 W for 4 minutes on cool, using 0.18 kWh. At $0.20/kWh, direct energy is about $0.036 per session. A longer low setting can use less or more than a short high setting depending on minutes.

## Compare real use cases

Compare quick high-heat drying, a diffuser at lower airflow, and two people sharing a bathroom outlet. Heat and fan settings change draw, while styling time changes the denominator; do not treat a 2,000 W label as the energy for every session.

## Limits and common mistakes

Hair length, moisture, towel drying, heat, airflow, attachments, pauses, voltage, thermostat cycling, and technique alter wall input. Follow manufacturer safety instructions and do not turn a cost estimate into a heat, hair, or health claim.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a higher wattage dryer always cost more per session?

Not necessarily. Runtime and setting matter. Compare measured kWh for the same drying outcome, record the minutes at each mode, and keep any styling or waiting time separate from electricity.
