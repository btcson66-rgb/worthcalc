# Hobby Equipment Cost Per Use: Include Storage, Maintenance, and Resale Risk

> **Quick answer:** Hobby equipment cost per use includes the item, accessories, maintenance, storage, lessons, and uses completed before interest fades.

## Collect the inputs that change the result

Enter equipment, accessories, safety gear, lessons, storage, maintenance, consumables, and likely resale. Count completed sessions, not the number of weekends available.

## Formula and worked example

cost per completed use = (equipment + accessories + upkeep + storage + lessons − realistic resale) ÷ completed uses. A $500 camera needs $140 accessories, $60 training, and $100 maintenance. At 40 planned shoots, cost is $20 per shoot before resale; at 12, it is $66.67.

## Use a range instead of a promise

Run a novelty-drop-off scenario as well as normal use. Ask whether borrowing, renting, or buying used would meet the first three months of the hobby. Run low-use, realistic-use, and high-use cases. If the result changes, show the range and the assumption that changes it. Do not treat “I may use it later” as a completed use. Safety equipment and necessary accessories are costs, not optional decorations.

Keep a short usage log for a month or cycle. Separate unavoidable costs from optional add-ons, and record why a planned use did not happen. That record makes the denominator auditable instead of optimistic.
