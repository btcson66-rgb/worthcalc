# Humidifier Electricity Cost Per Humidifying Hour Used: Measure Mist and Fan Modes

> **Quick answer:** Measure the humidifier at the mist level and fan mode you actually use, including automatic sensor cycles. Divide the electricity charge by completed humidifying hours, not by the tank’s advertised capacity. This is a cost estimate, not a saving, comfort, health, safety, performance, or income guarantee.

## Inputs that change the answer

Record humidifier type, mist setting, fan speed, target humidity, sensor mode, water refill, active hours, standby hours, measured kWh, tariff, and date. Keep a dehumidifier or HVAC outside the humidifier boundary. Compare low mist, high mist, and automatic humidity control. A tank can last longer without using less electricity if the device cycles differently, so record both runtime and wall kWh.

## Formula and measured example

electricity cost per completed humidifying hour = measured kWh for a humidifier × delivered electricity rate ÷ completed units. If the humidifier uses 0.025 kWh during 2.5 active hours at 0.20 per kWh, the charge is 0.005, or 0.002 per humidifying hour. Refill effort and water cost are separate inputs. Rated power or product labels can help plan a test; a reliable answer keeps wall-meter start and end times, kWh, tariff, and date.

## Limits and common mistakes

Room size, humidity, water quality, fan speed, mist technology, sensor calibration, filter, HVAC interaction, and standby time affect readings. Electricity cost is not proof of health, comfort, or humidity control. Run at least three repeatable measurements, keep the device boundary and tariff fixed, and preserve raw kWh plus the operating definition when comparing modes.

## Frequently asked question

### Can I divide by tank capacity?

No. Capacity is not completed humidifying time. Use measured runtime or a declared volume-based unit, and do not mix the two.
