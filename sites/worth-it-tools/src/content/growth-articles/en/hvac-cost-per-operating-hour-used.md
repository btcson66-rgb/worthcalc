# HVAC Cost Per Operating Hour Used: Make Runtime and Seasonal Rates Comparable

> **Quick answer:** Estimate an HVAC operating-hour cost from measured runtime, electrical demand, fuel, local rates, fixed charges, maintenance, and equipment allocation; a thermostat setpoint is not a universal hourly price.

## Inputs that change the result

Collect runtime or interval kWh, gas therms if applicable, cooling or heating mode, outdoor conditions, rate plan, delivery charges, filter and service costs, purchase price, and useful life.

## Formula and worked example

effective HVAC hour cost = (energy + delivery charges + maintenance + equipment allocation) ÷ observed operating hours. If a month has $120 energy, $20 delivery, $15 maintenance, and $45 equipment allocation over 100 observed runtime hours, the effective cost is $200 ÷ 100 = $2 per hour. Keep heating and cooling periods separate.

## Compare real use cases

Compare mild and extreme-weather weeks, not just thermostat settings. Duct leakage, insulation, humidity, short cycling, fan-only time, auxiliary heat, and installation quality can change runtime and delivered comfort. Treat comfort as an outcome, not a promised savings claim.

## Limits and common mistakes

ENERGY STAR says heating and cooling represent a major home energy use and that equipment performance depends on proper installation. Product metrics such as SEER2, HSPF2, and EER2 are test measures, not your exact hourly bill.
