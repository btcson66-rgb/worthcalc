# LED Lighting Cost Per Hour Used: Include Watts, Fixtures, and Replacement Life

> **Quick answer:** Calculate a light's hourly cost from actual wattage, hours, electricity rate, fixture count, replacement cost, and useful life; “low energy” is not a household price until usage is measured.

## Inputs that change the result

Record watts per bulb or fixture, number of lights, hours per day, days used, rate, delivery charges, dimmer or control losses if known, purchase price, rated life, and replacement frequency.

## Formula and worked example

effective lighting cost per hour = (watts ÷ 1000 × hours × electricity rate + replacement allocation + delivery charges) ÷ hours. Four 9-watt bulbs used for 5 hours consume 0.18 kWh. At $0.20 per kWh, energy is $0.036 for that period before fixed charges and replacement allocation. Compare a room, a fixture, and a whole-home month separately.

## Compare real use cases

Compare the same light output rather than only bulb wattage. ENERGY STAR explains that LEDs can be much more efficient than incandescent lighting and that rated life concerns lumen depreciation; brightness, controls, heat, and actual switching patterns still matter.

## Limits and common mistakes

A product page or rated life is not a promise of your exact bill or replacement date. EIA electricity figures and your utility bill use a rate basis; include only charges you can attribute consistently.
