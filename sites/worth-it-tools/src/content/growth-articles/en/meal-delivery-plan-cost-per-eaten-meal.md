# Meal Delivery Plan Cost Per Eaten Meal: Include Skipped and Replaced Orders

> **Quick answer:** Measure a meal-delivery plan by meals actually eaten, adding delivery, service fees, minimums, groceries bought as replacements, and active renewal.

## Collect the inputs that change the result

Record plan price, delivery, service fees, minimum order, taxes, add-ons, meals delivered, meals eaten, replacement groceries, skipped boxes, and renewal date.

## Formula and worked example

effective cost per eaten meal = (plan + delivery + fees + replacement groceries) ÷ meals actually eaten. A $48 box plus $6 delivery and $4 replacement groceries delivers four meals, but only two are eaten. Effective cost is $29 per eaten meal, not $13.50.

## Use a range instead of a promise

Run a normal week, busy week, and skipped-delivery week. Compare cooking, takeaway, and pause or cancel timing using the same meal count. Run low-use, realistic-use, and high-use cases. If the result changes, show the range and the assumption that changes it. Do not count a delivered meal that becomes waste or a replacement purchase as value. Check renewal and skip deadlines before relying on “pause anytime.”

Keep a short use log for a month or cycle. Separate unavoidable costs from optional extras, record missed uses, and check the current terms before the next charge. This makes the denominator auditable instead of optimistic.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can everyone use the same threshold?

No. Prices, usage, timing, and terms differ. Replace the example with your records and keep low, base, and high cases visible.

### What should I check first?

Is meal delivery cheaper than cooking? Compare eaten meals and replacement purchases with the home-cooking or takeaway alternative you would actually use. Recalculate when terms, price, or usage change.
