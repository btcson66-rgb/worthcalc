# Microwave Cost Per Cooking Cycle Used: Count Wattage, Time, and Failed Reheats

> **Quick answer:** Estimate the cost of a microwave cooking cycle from measured input power, active minutes, standby draw, batch size, electricity rates, cleaning, and edible output rather than treating the nameplate wattage as the cycle cost.

## Inputs that change the result

Record input watts, cooking and defrost minutes, standby time attributable to the session, batch size, rewarmed or discarded food, electricity rate, delivery charges, cleaning, purchase price, and cycles that produced usable food.

## Formula and worked example

effective usable-food cycle cost = (active energy + attributable standby + delivery charges + cleaning and equipment allocation) ÷ usable cycles. A cycle is only comparable when the food amount, starting temperature, container, and outcome are similar. A 1.2 kW microwave runs for 8 minutes and uses 0.16 kWh. At $0.20/kWh, direct energy is $0.03. Add $0.08 for allocated cleaning and equipment, then a successful cycle costs $0.11; two repeat reheats for one meal should be shown as three cycles, not one perfect cycle.

## Compare real use cases

Compare a single refrigerated portion, a frozen portion requiring defrost, and a family batch that needs a second round. CFPB notes that household utility costs vary with local rates and home characteristics; use your bill or measured tariff rather than a web-wide average.

## Limits and common mistakes

Cooking wattage is input power, not necessarily food heating delivered to the batch. Containers, moisture, turntable load, pauses, ventilation, and failed or uneven heating change useful output. Do not claim a microwave is always cheaper without matching the serving and result.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I count a reheat as another cycle?

Yes, when it is a separate run needed to make the food usable. Track cycle count and useful servings separately so a large batch, one serving, and repeated reheating are not mixed into one average.
