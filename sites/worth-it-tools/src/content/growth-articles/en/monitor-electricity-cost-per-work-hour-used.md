# Monitor Electricity Cost Per Work Hour Used: Include Brightness, Sleep, and USB Power

> **Quick answer:** Calculate a monitor's cost per work hour from measured on-mode and sleep-mode power, brightness, USB-C charging, electricity rates, and the hours it supports useful work instead of treating screen size as consumption.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure wall watts at the default brightness, brighter task, sleep, and off modes. Record USB-C power delivery, connected laptop or dock, work hours, idle time, electricity rate, delivery charges, purchase price, and useful life.

## Formula and worked example

effective work-hour monitor cost = (on-mode energy + sleep energy + USB power allocation + delivery charges + equipment allocation) ÷ useful work hours. Separate the monitor from the computer so a dual-screen setup can be compared honestly. A display draws 32 W for 7 work hours and 0.5 W for 2 idle-sleep hours, using 0.225 kWh. At $0.20/kWh, direct energy is $0.05 per workday. If it also supplies 45 W to a laptop, measure that output and input loss rather than assigning the laptop energy to the panel.

## Compare real use cases

Compare a bright design workload, ordinary office documents, and a monitor that sleeps during long breaks. ENERGY STAR's display criteria use on and sleep measurements and allowances for features; screen area and resolution alone are not your hourly bill.

## Limits and common mistakes

Brightness control, HDR, refresh rate, USB devices, speakers, network features, power conversion, and the connected computer affect wall input. Do not use a monitor's USB-C output rating as its own consumption, and do not treat a sleep-mode limit as a measured household value.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should dual monitors double the cost?

Only if both monitors have the same measured power and schedule. Measure each panel, include different brightness and sleep behavior, and divide by the work hours that actually benefited from the second screen.

## Source reading

Read the [ENERGY STAR monitor guidance](https://www.energystar.gov/products/monitors) and [EIA electricity explanation](https://www.eia.gov/tools/faqs/faq.php?id=507). They describe mode-based measurement and delivered electricity, not a universal screen-size rate.
