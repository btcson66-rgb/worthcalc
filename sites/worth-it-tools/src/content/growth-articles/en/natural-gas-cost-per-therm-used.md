# Natural Gas Cost Per Therm Used: Convert the Bill Before Comparing

> **Quick answer:** Calculate your delivered natural-gas cost per therm from the bill, keeping supply, delivery, taxes, fixed charges, and the billed therm or CCF conversion visible.

## Collect the inputs that change the result

Record therms, CCF or cubic feet, the bill period, supply and delivery charges, taxes, fixed customer charges, credits, and any gas used by more than one appliance. If the bill uses CCF, find the utility heat-content factor instead of assuming every CCF equals the same energy.

## Formula and worked example

effective cost per therm = (supply + delivery + taxes + fixed charges − applicable credits) ÷ billed therms. Suppose supply is $42, delivery is $31, fixed charges are $16, taxes are $5, and a $4 credit applies to a bill showing 20 therms. The effective cost is $90 ÷ 20 = $4.50 per therm. Comparing only the supply line would understate the cost paid to receive the service.

## Compare weather and billing periods carefully

Compare a mild month, an ordinary heating month, and a cold month using the actual therms and bill days. A fixed charge raises the unit cost in a low-use month; a longer billing period can make the bill look higher without changing the daily rate. If gas serves heating, water heating, or cooking, split the use only when you have a defensible meter or appliance estimate.

EIA explains that natural gas may be priced per therm, MMBtu, Ccf, or cubic foot, and that heat content can vary by location and over time. Do not copy a national average or a different utility rate. A lower therm price also does not prove a lower total household energy cost if usage, equipment, or service charges changed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a lower price per therm prove that gas is cheaper?

Not by itself. A lower unit rate can be offset by higher usage, delivery charges, fixed fees, or equipment differences. Compare the same billing period and keep service reliability separate.

### When should I recalculate?

How do I calculate my real natural-gas cost per therm? Use the therms billed for the same service period, add the charges required to receive the gas, subtract credits that reduced that bill, and divide by billed therms. Recheck the heat-content conversion and bill days whenever the utility changes its format.
