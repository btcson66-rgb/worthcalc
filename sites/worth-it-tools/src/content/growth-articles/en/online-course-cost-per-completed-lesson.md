# Online Course Cost Per Completed Lesson: Price Progress, Not Access

> **Quick answer:** An online course is cheaper per completed lesson when tuition, platform fees, books, and deadline extensions are spread over lessons you finish.

## Collect the inputs that change the result

Record tuition, financing or platform fees, books, equipment, deadline extensions, refunds, lesson count, lessons completed, and study hours.

## Formula and worked example

cost per completed lesson = (tuition + required materials + fees + extensions) ÷ lessons completed. A $240 course has $30 materials and $20 extension fees. Finishing 12 lessons costs $24.17 each; finishing 4 costs $72.50 each.

## Use a range instead of a promise

Model completion at one-third, two-thirds, and full lessons. Compare the course with a library, free syllabus, or shorter class based on finished learning tasks, not access promised. Run low-use, realistic-use, and high-use cases. If the result changes, show the range and the assumption that changes it. Do not call an unopened course an investment with zero cost. Check refund windows, subscription renewal, and whether the certificate is actually required.

Keep a short use log for a month or project. Separate unavoidable costs from optional extras, record missed uses, and check the current terms before the next charge. This makes the denominator auditable instead of optimistic.
