# Parking Pass Cost Per Day Used: Include Days You Work Remotely

> **Quick answer:** A parking pass is cheaper per used day only when the pass fee, reservation, fuel, and walking costs are divided by days you actually park.

## Collect the inputs that change the result

Enter pass price, taxes, reservation fees, fuel or transit to the lot, walking time, employer subsidy, workdays, remote days, holidays, and actual parking days.

## Formula and worked example

effective parking cost per used day = (pass + fees + access costs − certain subsidy) ÷ actual parking days. A $220 monthly pass has $15 fees and $25 fuel. At 20 parking days it costs $13 per day; at 8 days after remote work, it costs $32.50 per used day.

## Use a range instead of a promise

Model office-heavy, normal, and remote-heavy months. Compare the pass with daily parking and transit using the same work calendar and cash-flow dates. Run low-use, realistic-use, and high-use cases. If the result changes, show the range and the assumption that changes it. Do not divide by the lot’s open days or planned office days if remote work, leave, or carpooling reduces use. A subsidy may have tax or eligibility conditions.

Keep a short use log for a month or project. Separate unavoidable costs from optional extras, record missed uses, and check the current terms before the next charge. This makes the denominator auditable instead of optimistic.

### Can everyone use the same threshold?

No. Prices, usage, timing, and terms differ. Replace the example with your records and keep low, base, and high cases visible.

### What should I check first?

When is a parking pass worth it? Compare its total monthly cash cost with actual parking days and the realistic daily alternative. Recalculate when terms, price, or usage change.
