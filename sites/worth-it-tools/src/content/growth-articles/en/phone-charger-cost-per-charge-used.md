# Phone Charger Cost Per Charge Used: Measure the Wall Energy, Not the Adapter Maximum

> **Quick answer:** Calculate a phone-charging session cost from measured wall energy, battery state, charging losses, charger idle power, electricity rates, and charge frequency instead of using the adapter wattage as if it were continuous.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure wall kWh for a repeatable charge window, noting starting and ending battery percentage, cable, adapter, phone temperature, fast-charge setting, and whether other ports are active. Record the rate and the number of charges per week.

## Formula and worked example

cost per phone charge = wall kWh for the chosen battery increase × delivered electricity rate. Monthly cost = cost per charge × comparable charges, with idle charger energy reported separately when it remains plugged in. A measured session from 20% to 90% uses 0.018 kWh at the wall. At $0.20/kWh, energy is $0.0036, or about a third of a cent. Actual losses and battery capacity vary; repeat several sessions rather than presenting the adapter label as the result.

## Compare real use cases

Compare a slow overnight charge, fast charging before travel, and a multi-port charger powering a phone plus earbuds. EIA describes electricity use and price as inputs; the measured wall session determines this device result, not a universal phone wattage.

## Limits and common mistakes

Battery health, heat, screen use, fast-charge curves, cable quality, adapter efficiency, wireless charging, and chargers left connected change wall energy. Do not equate battery energy with wall energy, and do not promise a meaningful bill reduction from a charger swap without measurement.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does fast charging cost more?

It can change losses and timing, but the answer depends on the measured wall energy for the same battery increase. Compare like-for-like charge percentages and include the charger and cable in both tests.
