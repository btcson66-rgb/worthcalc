# Pool Pump Cost Per Operating Hour Used: Separate Speed, Flow, and Pool Tasks

> **Quick answer:** Calculate a pool pump's cost per operating hour from measured watts at each speed, filtration and cleaning schedules, electricity rates, maintenance, and equipment allocation instead of assuming every run hour uses the same energy.

## Inputs that change the result

Record watts or kWh at each speed, run minutes for filtration, heating, cleaning, and water features, pool size, season, electricity rate, delivery charges, filter care, repairs, purchase price, and useful life.

## Formula and worked example

effective pool-pump hour cost = (speed-specific energy + delivery charges + filter and maintenance allocation + equipment allocation) ÷ useful operating hours. A variable-speed pump needs a weighted average; nameplate horsepower alone is not consumption. A pump uses 1.0 kW for 4 filtration hours and 2.4 kW for 1 cleaning hour, or 6.4 kWh total. At $0.20/kWh, energy is $1.28. Across 5 useful hours, that is $0.26 per operating hour; reporting $0.20 from the low-speed mode would omit the cleaning task.

## Compare real use cases

Compare low-speed filtration, high-speed cleaning, and a water-feature schedule. ENERGY STAR says certified pumps can vary speed to match pool functions; that design fact does not predict your hydraulic load, schedule, or tariff.

## Limits and common mistakes

Do not use a single advertised horsepower or an ENERGY STAR savings example as your household bill. Plumbing resistance, filter pressure, pool size, water temperature, leaks, required turnover, and local rates can change run time and watts.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should cleaning and water features use separate rates?

Yes. Keep each task visible, then calculate a weighted average only when you need a single seasonal figure. This shows whether cost comes from circulation time, high-speed cleaning, heating support, or decorative features.
