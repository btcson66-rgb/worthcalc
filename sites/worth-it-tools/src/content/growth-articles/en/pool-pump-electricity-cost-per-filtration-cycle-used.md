# Pool Pump Electricity Cost Per Filtration Hour Used: Separate Run Time

> **Quick answer:** Measure pool-pump cost per filtration hour by separating speed, timer, priming, and standby states.

## Inputs that change the result

A pool pump may draw different power during priming, low-speed circulation, high-speed cleaning, and timer transitions. The meaningful denominator is a completed filtration hour under a stated flow or operating condition, not simply the time the motor was switched on. Record the device state, start and stop readings, measured kWh, delivered rate, and exact window. Define one completed unit before calculating, and list any shared outlet load rather than assuming it is zero.

## Formula and measured example

Cost per completed filtration hour = measured kWh × delivered electricity rate ÷ completed filtration hours. A measured 0.9 kWh at 0.20 per kWh over 3 completed low-speed filtration hours gives 0.06 per hour. It is an illustrative measurement example, not a typical pool cost. Keep the raw readings, currency, date, and rate beside the result so another person can reproduce it. A rounded value without its measurement boundary is not an auditable household cost.

## Compare real-use scenarios

Keep low-speed filtration, high-speed cleaning, and a priming interval as separate scenarios. Record whether the heater, salt system, cleaner, lights, or booster pump shared the meter boundary. ## Measurement method

Record water level, filter pressure, pump speed, timer window, priming time, measured kWh, rate, and the start/end readings. Repeat after the system reaches normal pressure. A blocked filter or changed valve position can make two hours non-comparable. If the load is estimated rather than measured, label the assumption and show how it changes the result. Repeat comparable windows, but do not erase a real operating difference just to make the numbers look tidy.

## Limits and common mistakes

Filtration quality, water chemistry, maintenance, and equipment life are not proved by a lower electricity number. Do not infer annual cost from one short run, and do not compare a pool system with different attached loads as if it were the same pump. Keep electricity separate from maintenance, water, consumables, repair, and any claimed outcome. At least three comparable readings can reveal variation, but repetition does not turn a small sample into a universal product claim.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the cheapest pump hour always the best filtration choice?

No. A low-speed hour may use less electricity but may not represent the same flow or completed filtration task. Compare measured kWh with the stated operating condition and keep water-care decisions separate.

### When should I recalculate?
