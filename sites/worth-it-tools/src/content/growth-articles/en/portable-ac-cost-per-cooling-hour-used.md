# Portable AC Cost Per Cooling Hour Used: Measure Exhaust, Fan, and Room Load

> **Quick answer:** Estimate a portable air conditioner's cost per occupied cooling hour from measured input, compressor cycling, fan-only time, electricity rates, exhaust setup, maintenance, and room load instead of treating the BTU label as a bill.

## Inputs that change the result

Record input watts or kWh, compressor-on minutes, fan-only minutes, room occupancy, outdoor temperature, humidity, exhaust arrangement, electricity rate, delivery charges, filter care, drainage, purchase price, and useful life.

## Formula and worked example

effective occupied cooling-hour cost = (compressor energy + fan energy + delivery charges + maintenance + equipment allocation) ÷ occupied cooling hours. If the unit runs while the room is empty, report that energy separately rather than hiding it in a comfort-hour denominator. A unit averages 1.1 kW for 5 compressor-equivalent hours and 0.08 kW for 3 fan-only hours, using 5.74 kWh. At $0.20/kWh, energy is $1.15 before fixed charges and upkeep. If the room was occupied for 8 hours, energy is $0.14 per occupied hour; the compressor-only figure would answer a different question.

## Compare real use cases

Compare a sealed exhaust hose on a mild day, a hot humid day, and a day with frequent empty-room fan operation. ENERGY STAR's room-air-conditioner criteria describe eligible room units and efficiency measures; they do not turn a portable unit's label into your local hourly rate.

## Limits and common mistakes

Portable air conditioners are not automatically covered by the room-air-conditioner certification criteria. Do not use a room AC CEER value as a portable-unit measurement. Exhaust leaks, condensate handling, filter condition, room size, sun exposure, and local tariffs can change the result.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I calculate the cost from the portable AC BTU rating?

Not reliably. BTU capacity describes cooling output under a test context, while the bill follows electrical input and actual cycling. Use a plug meter or interval kWh when possible, and keep capacity as a comparison field rather than a price input.
