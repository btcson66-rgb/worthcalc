# Refrigerator Cost Per Day Used: Turn Annual Energy Into Your Household Cost

> **Quick answer:** Estimate a refrigerator's daily cost from its measured energy use, local rate, fixed charges, service, and purchase allocation instead of treating an annual label estimate as your bill.

## Inputs that change the result

Record the label's annual kWh, your billed energy rate, fixed delivery charges, service or filter costs, purchase price, expected years, and whether a second fridge or freezer is actually used every day.

## Formula and worked example

effective refrigerator cost per day = (annual kWh × effective energy rate + annual service + annual equipment allocation) ÷ days used. A model rated at 500 kWh per year at $0.18 per kWh uses $90 of energy. Add $30 service and $180 of annualized equipment cost, then divide $300 by 365: about $0.82 per day. That is a planning example, not a universal price.

## Compare real use cases

Compare the label estimate with a plug meter or utility interval data when available. Door openings, ambient heat, ice making, defrost, seals, size, and whether the appliance is a second unit can move actual use. Compare cost per day and cost per refrigerated cubic foot separately.

## Limits and common mistakes

ENERGY STAR criteria compare measured energy against standards and product pages note that size, configuration, defrost, and ice-maker features affect consumption. A certified model is not a promise that your tariff, room temperature, or usage will match the test.

## Frequently asked questions

### Not by itself. Use the same rate basis and include purchase allocation, service, fixed charges, and the useful days of the appliance. A smaller or better-rated appliance can still have a different household cost if your usage pattern changes.

For a fair daily comparison, measure a complete 24-hour window and record the refrigerator's cycling behavior. Do not multiply the nameplate rating by a full day unless the compressor actually runs continuously; use the measured kWh instead.
