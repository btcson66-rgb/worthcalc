# Robot Vacuum Cost Per Cleaning Cycle Used: Include Docking, Charging, and Emptying

> **Quick answer:** Calculate a robot vacuum electricity cost per completed cleaning cycle from measured wall energy for cleaning, navigation, charging, and dock functions rather than dividing a battery label by an assumed number of runs.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure the wall kWh from a full or repeatable starting state through cleaning, return-to-dock, recharge, and any automatic emptying or washing. Record floor area, map, battery percentage, mop mode, dock idle watts, and how many rooms count as one cycle.

## Formula and worked example

cost per completed cycle = wall kWh from cycle start through recharge and attributable dock energy × delivered electricity rate. Define whether a cycle means one scheduled run, one room, or one full recharge so the denominator matches the task. A robot uses 0.045 kWh for a scheduled run and recharge, while its dock adds 0.6 W for the remaining 23 hours: total about 0.059 kWh per day. At $0.20/kWh, direct energy is about $0.012 per cycle-day before replacement bags, water, or parts.

## Compare real use cases

Compare a small apartment map, a carpet-heavy map that returns to charge, and a mop cycle with a self-cleaning dock. A daily scheduled run can have a different cost per cleaned room from an occasional deep run; report map and dock behavior rather than just battery capacity.

## Limits and common mistakes

Floor area, obstacles, suction, mop heating, return trips, battery age, dock pumps, automatic emptying, firmware, and missed areas alter energy. Do not count a dock’s maximum adapter rating as daily draw, and do not describe the device as cleaning a particular area unless your map and test support it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I include the charging dock?

Yes for the household system cost. Measure or allocate dock idle, recharge, emptying, and washing energy; show the robot run and dock services separately when comparing models or schedules.
