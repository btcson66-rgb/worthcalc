# Router Electricity Cost Per Month Used: Separate Always-On Power from Internet Service

> **Quick answer:** Estimate a router and modem setup cost per month from measured watts, uptime, backup equipment, electricity rates, and the service it keeps available; do not confuse electricity with the internet bill.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure the wall input of the modem, router, mesh nodes, optical terminal, and backup battery separately or as a complete stack. Record uptime, night shutdowns, guest devices, network storage, electricity rate, delivery charges, and whether a backup supports security or work.

## Formula and worked example

monthly router electricity cost = measured average watts × uptime hours ÷ 1,000 × delivered electricity rate. Add each always-on node and allocate backup losses separately; keep the internet subscription outside the electricity total. A 12 W modem-router stack running 24 hours for 30 days uses 8.64 kWh. At $0.20/kWh, direct energy is about $1.73 per month. A mesh node, optical terminal, or battery backup changes the stack, so measure the complete wall input before comparing plans.

## Compare real use cases

Compare one gateway in a small apartment, a gateway plus two mesh nodes in a larger home, and a setup that shuts down overnight. EIA explains that household electricity use depends on equipment and time, while the delivered rate comes from the local bill; neither source supplies a universal router wattage.

## Limits and common mistakes

Wi-Fi traffic, radio bands, USB ports, cooling, firmware, mesh backhaul, backup conversion losses, and automatic schedules change draw. Shutting off networking may affect cameras, alarms, calls, updates, or work. Do not call a lower bill guaranteed from a timer alone.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should the internet subscription be included?

No when the question is electricity cost. Show the subscription as a separate connectivity cost, then optionally combine both for a full monthly network budget so the two different cost drivers remain visible.
