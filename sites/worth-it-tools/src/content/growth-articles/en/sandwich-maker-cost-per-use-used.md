# Sandwich Maker Cost Per Use Used: Count Preheat and Actual Plates

> **Quick answer:** Calculate a sandwich maker electricity cost per use from measured preheat, cooking, and standby energy for the plates you actually use, rather than applying the nameplate watts to an assumed hour.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure wall kWh from the plug-in start through the last sandwich or waffle-style plate in the batch. Record preheat minutes, thermostat cycling, plate count, cooking minutes, open-lid pauses, and whether one or several servings share the heat-up.

## Formula and worked example

cost per use = measured kWh for the defined batch × delivered electricity rate. Cost per sandwich = batch electricity ÷ sandwiches finished; include a separate line for a one-sandwich cold start versus a multi-sandwich batch. A sandwich maker uses 0.025 kWh to heat and 0.035 kWh cooking four sandwiches, or 0.060 kWh for the batch. At $0.20/kWh, electricity is $0.012 per batch and $0.003 per sandwich before bread, fillings, and cleaning.

## Compare real use cases

Compare one sandwich from a cold start, four sandwiches back-to-back, and a batch with long open-lid pauses. A larger batch spreads preheat across more servings, but extra reheats or a long idle interval can reverse the difference.

## Limits and common mistakes

Plate material, thermostat cycling, batter or filling moisture, contact time, lid opening, voltage, surface temperature, and cleaning method change energy. The result is appliance electricity only; it does not establish cooking quality, safety, or ingredient value.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I allocate preheat to every sandwich?

Allocate one measured preheat across all sandwiches completed in the same uninterrupted batch. Keep the cold-start result separate when comparing occasional use, because a single sandwich bears more of that fixed setup energy.
