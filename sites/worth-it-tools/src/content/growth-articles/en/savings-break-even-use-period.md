# Savings Break-Even Period: How Many Months or Uses Make a Switch Worth It?

> **Quick answer:** break-even periods = one-time switching cost ÷ saving per month or use. Round up to the next whole period, then compare that threshold with your realistic time or usage. If the saving is zero or negative, there is no break-even point.

## The formula in context

Suppose a new service saves $14 each month but costs $84 to start. `$84 ÷ $14 = 6`, so it begins to recover the switching cost after six paid months. If you expect to leave after four months, the lower monthly price has not paid back the fee. Include only non-refundable costs; a refundable deposit affects cash timing rather than permanent cost.

For per-use plans, divide by saving per use. A reusable filter costs $72 and saves $4 per replacement, so the break-even threshold is 18 replacements. If you replace only 10 cartridges before the filter expires, the headline saving is not yet real.

## Add timing and uncertainty

Use low, typical, and high usage cases. A $120 installation cost with $20 monthly saving breaks even in six months, but an $8 saving needs 15 months. If the contract renews or the price changes at month 12, calculate the first-year result separately from later years.

## FAQ

### Do I round the break-even result?

Yes, round up to the next whole month or use because a partial period usually cannot recover the full fee.

### What if the new plan is sometimes more expensive?

Use an average saving only when the usage estimate is documented, and show a sensitivity range.

### Is break-even the same as profit?

No. Break-even recovers the switching cost; savings begin after that point.
