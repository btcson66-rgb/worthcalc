# Sewing Machine Electricity Cost Per Sewing Hour Used: Include Foot Pedal Time

> **Quick answer:** Use actual needle-running time as the sewing-hour denominator. Measure the motor, work light, controller, and short idle periods that belong to the job instead of dividing a bill by hours spent in the room. This is a cost estimate, not a guarantee of quality, health, safety, comfort, performance, or income.

## Inputs that change the answer

Record stitch speed, foot-pedal time, needle stops, work light, controller, measured kWh, tariff, and date. Keep cutting, ironing, and a powered-but-idle machine outside the sewing-hour denominator unless your job boundary includes them.

## Formula and measured example

If a sewing boundary uses 0.16 kWh during 4 completed sewing hours at 0.20 per kWh, cost is 0.032, or 0.008 per sewing hour. Light-only and motor-only readings reveal what drives the result. Formula: cost per completed unit = measured kWh × delivered electricity rate ÷ completed units.

## Limits and common mistakes

Fabric thickness, stitch speed, needle stops, lighting, motor type, controller, maintenance, and idle habits change readings. Electricity cost does not establish stitch quality, productivity, or repair needs. Run at least three repeatable measurements, keep the device boundary and tariff fixed, and preserve raw kWh plus the operating definition when comparing modes.

## Frequently asked question

### Does the sewing light belong in the cost?

Include it for a full sewing setup. For motor cost, isolate the light and report it as a separate load.
