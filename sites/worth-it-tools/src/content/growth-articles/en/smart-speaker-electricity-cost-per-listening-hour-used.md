# Smart Speaker Electricity Cost Per Listening Hour Used: Separate Active and Standby

> **Quick answer:** Calculate smart speaker electricity cost per listening hour from measured playback and standby energy, keeping multi-room devices, chargers, and subscription costs separate. This is a cost estimate, not a savings, product-performance, ranking, or household-outcome guarantee.

## What changes the result

smart speaker cost per listening hour starts with a declared wall-meter boundary. Start and end with a declared wall-meter boundary. Record the device state, load, connected accessories, completed unit, tariff unit, and measurement date. A rating or typical value can plan a test but cannot replace a repeatable reading.

Playback level, voice assistant activity, Wi-Fi state, updates, and wake-word listening can change the wall reading. Decide whether the hour means continuous playback or a clock hour that includes pauses and standby.

## Formula and worked example

Electricity cost = measured wall kWh × delivered electricity rate. Unit cost = electricity cost ÷ completed units. Keep fixed fees, taxes, supplies, repairs, subscriptions, and the computer or network load outside the numerator unless your stated boundary intentionally includes them. If one listening hour measures 0.006 kWh at $0.18/kWh, the electricity cost is $0.00108. If two linked speakers are included, measure the pair together and label it as a multi-room result.

Do not multiply a rated wattage by the whole elapsed period unless the wall meter confirms that load. If a computer, display, network device, charger, or accessory is present, measure it separately before combining boundaries.

## Compare real use cases

Compare quiet playback, normal speech or music, and an idle hour. Keep volume, streaming source, assistant state, display lights, and connected USB accessories recorded.

## Limits and common mistakes

Voltage, tariff structure, firmware, room conditions, mode, load, measurement resolution, standby window, and failed work can change the result. Test at least three repeatable sessions, combine short runs when the meter resolution is coarse, and compare one changed condition at a time. The estimate is not a promise of savings, performance, or household results.
