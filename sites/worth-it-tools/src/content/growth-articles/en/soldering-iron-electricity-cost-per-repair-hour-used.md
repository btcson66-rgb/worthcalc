# Soldering Iron Electricity Cost Per Repair Hour Used: Include Warm-Up

> **Quick answer:** Define the repair boundary before measuring: warm-up, active soldering, temperature cycling, and cool-down if it remains powered. Divide its measured charge by completed repair hours, not by the project calendar. This is a cost estimate, not a guarantee of quality, health, safety, comfort, performance, or income.

## Inputs that change the answer

Record wattage mode, warm-up minutes, temperature setpoint, active tip time, sleep mode, measured kWh, tariff, and date. Keep a fume extractor or bench light separate unless the repair boundary explicitly includes it.

## Formula and measured example

A 0.09 kWh repair boundary at 0.20 per kWh costs 0.018. If it covers 3 completed repair hours, the cost is 0.006 per repair hour. Warm-up-only and active-use tests answer different questions. Formula: cost per completed unit = measured kWh × delivered electricity rate ÷ completed units.

## Limits and common mistakes

Tip temperature, thermal recovery, sleep controls, supply voltage, ventilation, cord losses, and operator pauses affect readings. Electricity cost does not establish joint quality, fume safety, or repair success. Run at least three repeatable measurements, keep the device boundary and tariff fixed, and preserve raw kWh plus the operating definition when comparing modes.

## Frequently asked question

### Should warm-up electricity be included?

Include it when the user must power the iron for the repair. Report it separately when comparing an already-hot working hour.
