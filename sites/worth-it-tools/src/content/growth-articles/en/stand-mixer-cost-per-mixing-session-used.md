# Stand Mixer Cost Per Mixing Session Used: Count Motor Time and Attachments

> **Quick answer:** Estimate a stand mixer electricity cost per mixing session from measured motor energy across mixing, kneading, and whipping settings rather than treating the motor rating as energy used for the whole recipe.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure the session from plug-in through the last powered step. Record attachment, speed, motor-on minutes, pauses for scraping, bowl load, dough stiffness, idle time, and the number of batches or finished portions.

## Formula and worked example

cost per mixing session = measured wall kWh for powered steps and included pauses × delivered electricity rate. Cost per batch or portion = session electricity divided by the batches or portions actually completed. A mixer uses 0.012 kWh for three minutes of whipping and 0.048 kWh for eight minutes of kneading, or 0.060 kWh total. At $0.20/kWh, direct electricity is $0.012 per session; the stand mixer may still have meaningful equipment and ingredient costs outside the formula.

## Compare real use cases

Compare a light whisking session, stiff dough at low speed, and two batches sharing one setup. Kneading can draw differently from whipping, while scraping and waiting change whether the denominator is a batch, a powered minute, or a finished portion.

## Limits and common mistakes

Attachment, speed, load, dough hydration, gearbox behaviour, pauses, bowl size, voltage, overload protection, and countertop setup alter input. Do not infer mixer durability, baking results, or labour savings from a small electricity figure.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should scraping pauses count?

Count pauses that are part of the real powered routine when they include the motor or idle energy you want to budget. Show motor-only and whole-session results if scraping or waiting is long enough to change the comparison.
