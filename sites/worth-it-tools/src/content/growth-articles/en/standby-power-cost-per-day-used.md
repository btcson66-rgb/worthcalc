# Standby Power Cost Per Day Used: Measure the Plug Load and the Service It Supports

> **Quick answer:** Estimate a device's standby cost per day from measured watts, always-on hours, network or clock functions, electricity rates, delivery charges, and the service the device provides rather than assuming every plugged-in watt is waste.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure idle or standby watts, hours in standby, active-use hours, sleep settings, network or display functions, electricity rate, delivery charges, number of identical devices, replacement cost, and whether the service is actually needed.

## Formula and worked example

standby cost per day = standby watts ÷ 1,000 × standby hours × delivered electricity rate + attributable fixed or equipment allocation. If a device cycles rather than drawing constant power, use interval energy or the average measured wattage. A media device averages 6 W for 24 hours, using 0.144 kWh. At $0.20/kWh, direct energy is $0.03 per day, or about $10.51 per 365-day year before other charges. Compare that with the value of instant access, updates, clock, or network availability instead of calling every dollar avoidable.

## Compare real use cases

Compare a powered-off device with network standby, a charger with no connected device, and a router that must remain on for other household services. EIA distinguishes electricity consumption data from a universal household rate; CFPB notes utility costs vary with local rates, climate, and home characteristics.

## Limits and common mistakes

A plug meter reading is specific to the device, mode, voltage, and time window. Do not extrapolate one model to an entire product category, and do not unplug equipment that supports safety, medical needs, security, refrigeration, or network access without checking the manual and household consequences.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is standby power always worth eliminating?

No. First measure it, then compare the annual cost with the service lost, replacement or timer cost, inconvenience, and any safety or data risk. A small measured load can be rational to keep when it supports a necessary shared service.
