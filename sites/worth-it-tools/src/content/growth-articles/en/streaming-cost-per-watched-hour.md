# Streaming Cost Per Watched Hour: Include Ads, Add-Ons, and Auto-Renewal

> **Quick answer:** A streaming plan’s effective cost depends on hours actually watched and on fees, ads, add-ons, and renewal terms that remain active.

## Collect the inputs that change the result

Enter monthly price, taxes, add-ons, ad-supported limits, household sharing, watched hours, free-trial end date, and the next renewal price.

## Formula and worked example

effective cost per watched hour = (subscription + taxes + add-ons) ÷ hours actually watched. A $18 plan plus a $3 add-on is watched for 7 hours in a month, so the effective cost is $3 per watched hour. At 24 hours it is $0.88.

## Use a range instead of a promise

Run a normal month, travel month, and month with a new release. Count hours you intentionally watched, and compare cancellation or pause timing with the next billing date. Run low-use, realistic-use, and high-use cases. If the result changes, show the range and the assumption that changes it. Do not assign value to a catalogue you did not watch or to a trial you forgot to cancel. Check current renewal and negative-option terms before relying on a reminder.

Keep a short use log for a month or project. Separate unavoidable costs from optional extras, record missed uses, and check the current terms before the next charge. This makes the denominator auditable instead of optimistic.
