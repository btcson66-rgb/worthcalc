# Television Electricity Cost Per Watched Hour Used: Include Picture Mode and Streaming Gear

> **Quick answer:** Estimate a television's cost per watched hour from measured on-mode and standby energy, picture settings, streaming equipment, electricity rates, and useful viewing hours instead of screen size alone.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure TV wall watts in default, bright, HDR, gaming, sleep, and network-standby modes. Record streaming box, soundbar, console, hours watched, idle time, rate and delivery charges, and whether the screen is shared by several viewers.

## Formula and worked example

effective watched-hour cost = (TV on energy + attributable standby + streaming and audio energy + delivery charges + equipment allocation) ÷ useful watched hours. State whether the denominator is viewing time or total powered-on time. A TV averages 90 W for 4 watched hours and 1 W for 20 standby hours, using 0.38 kWh. At $0.20/kWh, direct energy is $0.08 per day. Add a 12 W streaming box and 25 W soundbar only for their actual powered hours; a large screen is not automatically a higher cost per hour.

## Compare real use cases

Compare default picture mode, a bright HDR film, and gaming with a console and soundbar. ENERGY STAR television criteria distinguish on-mode and standby behavior; they do not include every external device or your local rate.

## Limits and common mistakes

Brightness, HDR, refresh rate, network activity, connected devices, automatic power-down, and content can change draw. Do not convert a published efficiency percentage into your bill, and do not count subscription fees as electricity cost in the same denominator.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should streaming devices be included?

Include them when the question is the cost of the complete viewing setup. Report the TV alone as a separate line so viewers can see whether the main screen, sound, or streaming hardware drives the result.
