# Terrarium Heat Lamp Electricity Cost Per Heating Hour Used: Count the Timer

> **Bottom line:** This is a measured cost estimate, not a product average and not a promise about comfort, safety, performance, health, or savings.

## Inputs that change the result

Write down the device state, the start and stop times, the measured kWh, the delivered electricity rate, and exactly what the plug or meter included. For this topic, record lamp wattage, timer schedule, thermostat state, enclosure size, room temperature, measured kWh, rate, and window. Define one completed unit as **completed heating hour** before reading the meter. If the equipment has a timer, thermostat, charger, light, or standby state, write down whether it is inside the boundary rather than assuming the label captures it.

## Formula and measured example

Cost per completed unit = measured kWh × delivered electricity rate ÷ completed units. Use the unit named in this page rather than silently converting it to hours, days, or cycles. If the measured window uses 0.18 kWh at a 0.20 per kWh rate, cost is 0.036; if it completes 3 completed heating hour, the result is 0.012 per unit. This is a formula example, not a product average. Keep the raw start and end readings, the rate currency, and the date beside the result. A rounded number without its measurement window is difficult to audit or reproduce.

## Measurement method

For a repeatable reading, use the same outlet boundary and rate, record the operating window, and keep the raw meter reading. A wall meter is more useful here than a label wattage because standby and control cycles can be material. Repeat the window under similar conditions, but do not erase real operating differences. Note lamp wattage, timer schedule, thermostat state, enclosure size, room temperature, measured kWh, rate, and window and identify which readings came from the wall meter, plug meter, or device display. If a load is estimated rather than measured, label it as an assumption and show how it affects the total.

## Limits and common mistakes

Keep equipment electricity separate from consumables, maintenance, water, materials, and any claimed outcome. A lower electricity cost is not proof of a better product or decision. Do not convert a rated wattage into a personal bill without accounting for run time, control cycles, standby, and your delivered rate. At least three repeatable readings can expose start-up or thermostat variation, but repetition does not turn a small sample into a universal product claim.
