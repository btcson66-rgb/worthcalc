# Transit Pass Cost Per Ride Used: Compare Trips, Transfers, and Missed Days

> **Quick answer:** Measure a transit pass by rides actually taken, including zone upgrades, transfers, reload fees, and days you do not commute.

## Collect the inputs that change the result

Record pass price, zones, reload or card fees, transfer charges, employer subsidy, commuting days, rides per day, missed trips, and the pay-as-you-go fare.

## Formula and worked example

cost per ride used = (pass + required fees − certain subsidy) ÷ rides actually taken. A $100 pass plus $5 card fee covers two rides on 20 commute days, so 40 rides cost $2.63 each. With 10 commute days, the same pass costs $5.25 per ride.

## Use a range instead of a promise

Test office-heavy, leave-heavy, and mixed-mode months. Include the second ride only when it is actually made; compare total trip time separately from cash cost. Run low-use, realistic-use, and high-use cases. If the result changes, show the range and the assumption that changes it. Do not treat unlimited access as unlimited value. If a pass causes extra zones or is abandoned during leave, those facts belong in the low-use case.

Keep a short use log for a month or project. Separate unavoidable costs from optional extras, record missed uses, and check the current terms before the next charge. This makes the denominator auditable instead of optimistic.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can everyone use the same threshold?

No. Prices, usage, timing, and terms differ. Replace the example with your records and keep low, base, and high cases visible.

### What should I check first?

How many rides make a transit pass worthwhile? Divide the full pass cost by rides you will actually take and compare with the current single-ride fare. Recalculate when terms, price, or usage change.
