# Home Service Plan True Cost: Price the Coverage You Actually Use

> **Quick answer:** The usable value of a home service plan depends on annual fees, dispatch charges, exclusions, repair limits, and the number of covered events you would otherwise pay for.

## Collect the numbers that change the decision

Enter annual premium, call-out fee, uncovered parts, repair cap, expected service calls, existing manufacturer or landlord coverage, and a self-funded repair reserve. Read the service contract as a set of conditional reimbursements. Count only repairs that are both likely enough to plan for and covered under the exact wording.

## Formula and worked example

effective cost per used event = annual fee + service fees + uncovered parts − reimbursements, divided by realistic covered events. A zero-claim year is not a failure; it is a high effective cost per use. A plan costs $600 a year, has two $75 dispatch fees, and covers one eligible repair with $300 reimbursement. Net cash cost is $450 before exclusions; with one event, effective cost is $450 per event.

## Use scenarios instead of one answer

Read the service contract as a set of conditional reimbursements. Count only repairs that are both likely enough to plan for and covered under the exact wording. Do not treat a plan limit as guaranteed value. Test denied claims, waiting periods, contractor choice, and whether a reserve would leave you with more flexible cash. Compare a low-use case, a realistic case, and a high-cost or high-use case. If the conclusion changes, report the range and the assumption that caused the change rather than presenting a universal recommendation. Write down the date and the source of each input. Recheck the result after a price change, a different usage pattern, or a contract renewal; a calculator is a comparison record, not a promise.

## Common mistakes and next step

Do not count a theoretical limit, a promotional headline, or a benefit you would not otherwise buy. Review the current terms for fees, exclusions, refunds, and timing. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can everyone use the same result?

No. Prices, usage, coverage, taxes, timing, and contract terms differ. Replace the example values with your own records and keep the low, base, and high cases visible.

### What is the most important check?

Is a home service plan the same as homeowners insurance? No. Their contracts, covered events, exclusions, and purposes differ; compare the documents, not the labels. Recalculate when the terms or usage change.
