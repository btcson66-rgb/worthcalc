# Unused Membership Capacity: Calculate the Cost of Benefits You Do Not Use

> **Quick answer:** effective cost per used benefit = total membership fee ÷ benefits you realistically use. Count expiry dates, blackout rules, and required add-ons before calling a large allowance “value”.

## Advertised capacity is not usage

A $360 membership includes 24 visits, but your diary shows 15 visits. Your effective cost is `$360 ÷ 15 = $24 per used visit`, not $15 based on the maximum. A smaller $255 plan with 15 visits costs $17 per used visit and may fit better even though its advertised limit is lower.

Use completed visits, deliveries, or redemptions from your own history. Do not count a benefit you would use only if the membership were free. If benefits roll over, use the contract rule; if they expire, assign unused capacity a value of zero unless you have a realistic plan to use it.

## Test the decision

Calculate low, typical, and high usage. At 10 visits, the $360 plan costs $36 each; at 20 visits, $18 each. Add travel, booking fees, or required upgrades if they differ by plan. Then compare the total first-year cost in the [annual cost savings calculator](/en/guides/annual-cost-savings-calculator/) and set the fee beside other commitments in the [budget builder](/en/tools/budget-builder/).

The [CFPB spending guidance](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/owning-a-home/prepare/assess-your-spending/) recommends reviewing several months of spending. That makes a measured usage estimate more defensible than a promotional forecast. This is educational budgeting information, not financial or legal advice.

## FAQ

### Should unused benefits have a negative value?

Usually treat them as zero, then disclose the expiry or opportunity cost separately rather than inventing a penalty.

### What if my usage will increase?

Show the higher-use case and the evidence for it. Do not let a hoped-for change be the only reason the plan wins.

### Is price per advertised unit useful?

Only as a secondary figure. Price per realistically used unit is the decision figure.
