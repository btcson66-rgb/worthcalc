# Vacuum Cleaner Electricity Cost Per Cleaning Hour Used: Measure the Real Session

> **Quick answer:** Estimate a vacuum cleaner electricity cost per cleaning hour from measured wall watts, actual cleaning time, power mode, and your delivered electricity rate instead of treating the motor label as constant use.

## Inputs that change the result

Measure wall watts in eco, standard, and high-power modes, including the hose, powered head, battery charger, or dock when the question is about the household setup. Record minutes of actual suction, pauses, floor type, rate, and any maintenance or replacement cost kept outside electricity.

## Formula and worked example

cleaning-hour electricity cost = average wall watts × active cleaning hours ÷ 1,000 × delivered electricity rate. If a cordless vacuum is charged between sessions, measure charger input for the battery increase and allocate it to the cleaning hours that battery supported. A corded vacuum averages 650 W for 35 active minutes, using about 0.379 kWh. At $0.20/kWh, direct electricity is about $0.08 for that session, or about $0.14 per active cleaning hour. A short high-power pass can cost more per hour than a longer low-power pass.

## Compare real use cases

Compare a quick hard-floor pass, a carpet session at high power, and a cordless model whose dock remains plugged in between uses. The same room can have different cost per hour when pauses, powered brushes, and modes change; report active cleaning time separately from the time the person spends tidying.

## Limits and common mistakes

Suction setting, clogged filters, brush resistance, floor type, battery charging losses, standby dock power, and user technique affect wall input. Do not infer a household bill from advertised motor watts, and do not include labor value or replacement parts unless you label them as separate costs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I divide by the whole time spent cleaning?

Use active powered minutes when comparing electricity intensity. You may also report a task-hour figure that includes pauses, but name the denominator clearly so a 20-minute powered session is not compared with an hour of household time without explanation.
