# Water Bill Cost Per Gallon Used: Include Sewer and Fixed Charges

> **Quick answer:** Calculate water cost from the gallons recorded on the bill, while keeping sewer, stormwater, fixed service charges, taxes, and leak-related use visible.

## Collect the inputs that change the result

Record water volume and unit, sewer charge, stormwater fee, fixed service fee, taxes, credits, meter dates, household occupants, outdoor irrigation, and any suspected leak. If the utility bills in CCF or another unit, convert it using the utility or EPA explanation before dividing.

## Formula and worked example

effective cost per gallon used = (water + sewer + stormwater + fixed charges + taxes − applicable credits) ÷ gallons used. A bill charges $28 for water, $34 for sewer, $8 in fixed fees, and $4 tax, with a $2 credit. If it records 10,000 gallons, the effective cost is $72 ÷ 10,000 = $0.0072 per gallon. Dividing only the water line would ignore most of the bill.

## Use a range instead of a promise

Compare an indoor-only month, a normal month, and a high outdoor-use month. Fixed charges make low-use months look expensive per gallon, while irrigation or a leak can raise the denominator without creating useful household value. Keep conservation changes, repair cost, and service reliability as separate scenarios.

EPA WaterSense explains that one CCF equals 748 gallons and that water use varies by household, weather, irrigation, and leaks. Do not copy an average household use or a different utility’s rate as your own. A lower bill after a leak repair is not proof that every fixture has the same unit cost.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a lower cost per gallon prove greater efficiency?

Not by itself. A low rate can reflect a high fixed charge spread over more gallons, while a high bill can reflect a leak or outdoor use. Review water volume and service quality separately.

### When should I recalculate?

How do I calculate my real water cost per gallon? Convert the bill volume to gallons, add all charges tied to that service period, subtract credits that reduced the bill, and divide by the recorded gallons. Recheck the next bill if the meter period or usage pattern changes.
