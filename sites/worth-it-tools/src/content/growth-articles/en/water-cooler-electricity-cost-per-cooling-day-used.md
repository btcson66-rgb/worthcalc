# Water Cooler Electricity Cost Per Cooling Day Used: Separate Hot and Cold Modes

> **Quick answer:** Measure the cooler across a defined day with cold-only, hot-and-cold, or on-demand modes identified. Divide the wall energy charge by completed cooling days, and separate water-draw energy from no-draw holding energy. This is a cost estimate, not a saving, comfort, health, safety, performance, or income guarantee.

## Inputs that change the answer

Record cooler type, hot/cold switches, storage or on-demand mode, water draws, draw volume, active hours, no-draw hours, measured kWh, tariff, and date. Use the same bottle or point-of-use setup when comparing tests. Run a no-draw day, then a normal draw day. ENERGY STAR water-cooler criteria distinguish categories and report both no-draw and draw performance; use that as context, but measure your model, room, and tariff.

## Formula and measured example

electricity cost per completed cooling day = measured kWh for a water cooler × delivered electricity rate ÷ completed units. If a cold-only cooler uses 0.16 kWh over a 24-hour test at 0.20 per kWh, the no-draw charge is 0.032 per day. Hot mode or repeated draws must be reported as a different boundary. Rated power or product labels can help plan a test; a reliable answer keeps wall-meter start and end times, kWh, tariff, and date.

Compare cold-only, hot-and-cold, frequent draws, and overnight settings. A calendar day and a day of active water service are not interchangeable denominators. ## Limits and common mistakes

Room temperature, bottle placement, setpoint, compressor cycle, hot tank, draw volume, maintenance, ventilation, and no-draw time change readings. Electricity cost is not a claim about water quality, temperature, or filtration. Run at least three repeatable measurements, keep the device boundary and tariff fixed, and preserve raw kWh plus the operating definition when comparing modes.

## Frequently asked question

### Should I use a product label daily number?

Use it for planning or comparison only. For a personal bill estimate, use your measured kWh and delivered tariff with the same mode.
