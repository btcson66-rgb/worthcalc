Enter every income stream and bill at its real frequency — weekly, monthly, quarterly, or annual — and this tool converts them all to one monthly view so irregular pay and once-a-year bills stop looking like surprises.

## Before you enter numbers

If your income varies, use your lowest recent months as your essentials floor rather than your average — see the companion article for why. List every non-monthly bill (insurance, subscriptions, annual fees) at its actual frequency; the tool converts it for you.

## Formula

`monthly equivalent = amount × occurrences per year ÷ 12`

## Worked example

A freelancer with income ranging $3,200–$6,800 a month can enter rent monthly, auto insurance every six months, and an annual software subscription. The tool converts all three to one monthly view and shows the true surplus against an income-floor budget, not an averaged one.

## Handling irregular income

Base essential expenses on a conservative income floor — closer to your worst recent months than your average — and treat anything earned above that floor as a deliberate allocation: tax savings, buffer contributions, or discretionary spending.

## Building a buffer first

If your income varies, most guidance points self-employed households toward the higher end of the typical multi-month expense-buffer range before relying heavily on percentage-based rules, since both income and its timing are uncertain. Build that cushion before fine-tuning category splits.

## Limits and privacy

This tool provides a general educational estimate, not individualized financial or tax advice. It does not calculate your tax liability — see the companion article for typical self-employment tax-reserve guidance and the IRS's own estimated-tax resources.

The calculation runs in your browser. Do not put account numbers or other identifying information into a shareable link.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I use gross or take-home income?

Use take-home income for day-to-day cash flow; keep gross income handy separately if you're also using a DTI-focused tool.

### How do I enter a bill I pay twice a year?

Enter the full amount with a frequency of 2 per year, and the tool divides it into a monthly sinking-fund contribution.

### Is 50/30/20 required?

No — it's an optional reference check, most useful when compared against your income floor rather than your average income.

### How should irregular income be handled?

Base essentials on a conservative income floor and treat income above that floor as a separate, deliberate allocation.

### Does savings count as an expense line?

Yes — treat it as a planned allocation with its own line, even though it builds assets rather than paying a bill.

A monthly budget should also reserve for predictable annual bills. Convert them into a monthly sinking-fund requirement.
