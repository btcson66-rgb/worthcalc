## A score is not worth anything by itself

Credit scores get treated as a scoreboard. They are a price tag, and the price only applies to money you borrow. The right question is never "how do I raise my score" in the abstract — it is "what will this score cost me on the specific borrowing I am about to do".

    value = Σ (interest at my tier − interest at the better tier), across the loans I will actually take

## Two prices, computed

**A car loan.** $35,000 over 60 months, priced across credit tiers:

| Tier | APR | Monthly | Total interest |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Strongest | 4.41% | $651.07 | $4,064 |
| Weakest | 16.11% | $853.17 | $16,190 |
| **Spread** | 11.70 pts | $202.10 | **$12,126** |

**A mortgage.** $400,000 over 30 years, one percentage point apart:

| Rate | Monthly P&I | Over 360 payments |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 5.95% | $2,385.42 | $858,751 |
| 6.95% | $2,647.79 | $953,204 |
| **Spread** | **$262.37** | **$94,453** |

For someone buying a car and a house in the next five years, the two together put a five-figure to six-figure price on the file. For someone who will not borrow at all in that period, the same improvement is worth close to nothing.

## Where else it shows up

Beyond interest, a thin or damaged file costs money in less visible ways:

- **Insurance premiums.** Many places permit a credit-based insurance score in pricing — a different model from a lending score, and regulated separately by state.
- **Deposits.** Utility, phone and rental deposits are frequently waived or reduced above a threshold, converting a score into immediate cash flow.
- **Rental applications.** A file that fails a landlord's screen can cost a larger deposit, a guarantor, or the apartment.

These are real but small next to the loan spreads. The loans are where the money is.

## What actually moves it, and how fast

1. **Revolving utilisation — fastest.** Utilisation is recomputed each time a statement reports. Paying balances down *before the statement date* rather than before the due date can show up within one or two cycles.
2. **Payment history — largest, slowest.** It cannot be accelerated, only protected. A single missed payment is expensive and stays on the file for years.
3. **Age of accounts — slow, and easy to damage.** Closing an old card removes its limit from the utilisation calculation immediately and its age eventually. A no-fee card left open and unused is usually doing useful work.
4. **New inquiries — small, and worth timing.** Rate-shopping a single loan inside a short window is generally treated as one event by scoring models; opening unrelated accounts before a mortgage application is not.

## What would reverse the conclusion

- **No borrowing planned.** If there is no loan in the next several years, the score's value is limited to insurance and deposits, and the effort is better spent elsewhere.
- **A subsidised rate.** Manufacturer 0% financing and some employer or member programmes price off eligibility rather than tier, which compresses the spread the score is worth.
- **A large down payment.** Lowering loan-to-value can move the pricing tier independently of the score, and on a mortgage it also removes mortgage insurance, which is frequently worth more than a small score gain.
- **Time pressure.** A score improvement takes cycles to report. If the purchase is in three weeks, the arithmetic is interesting but not actionable — pay down revolving balances, get the pre-approval, and move on.
