## The break-even, which is not the problem

    break-even visits per month = monthly fee ÷ drop-in price

| Monthly fee | Drop-in price | Break-even visits |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $30 | $15 | 2.0 |
| $55 | $15 | 3.7 |
| $55 | $22 | 2.5 |
| $85 | $25 | 3.4 |
| $150 (boutique) | $32 | 4.7 |

Under five visits a month in every row — roughly once a week. On that test almost every membership is correctly priced, and the comparison sites that stop here conclude that memberships are good value. They are, conditional on an assumption they do not check.

## The assumption

The variable that decides the outcome is not the price, it is the visit count, and it is the one input almost nobody estimates well about themselves. The pattern is familiar enough to plan around: the attendance people sign up for is a few times a week, and the attendance they sustain is closer to once.

    effective cost per visit = monthly fee ÷ visits actually made

| Visits in a month | Effective cost per visit, $55 membership |
| ---: | ---: |
| 12 | $4.58 |
| 8 | $6.88 |
| 4 | $13.75 |
| 2 | $27.50 |
| 1 | $55.00 |
| 0 | — |

At twelve visits the membership is excellent. At four it is $13.75 a visit, which is roughly the drop-in rate it was supposed to beat. At one or zero it is a standing order with no counterparty performance at all.

The spread matters more than the average. Gym attendance does not cluster tidily around four a month; it tends to separate into people who keep going and people who stopped in week six. An average taken across both describes neither, so the useful question is which group you will be in — not what the typical member does.

## Pricing the exit

A membership's real cost includes what it takes to leave, and that is not in the monthly figure:

| Line | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Joining fee | $99 |
| Monthly fee × 12-month minimum term | $660 |
| Annual maintenance charge | $49 |
| **First-year total** | **$808** |
| **Effective monthly** | **$67.33** |

At $67.33 the break-even rises to 4.5 visits a month. And if the minimum term cannot be exited early, the cost of stopping in month three is not $55 a month saved — it is the remaining nine months, still owed.

That structure has attracted regulatory attention for a reason. Check the cancellation terms before joining, not when you want out, because the difficulty of leaving is a feature of the price rather than an accident of the paperwork.

## What would reverse the conclusion

- **A documented habit.** If you already trained three times a week somewhere else for the last three months, the forecast problem disappears and the membership is straightforwardly the cheapest option available.
- **No drop-in alternative.** Where a venue sells no per-visit rate, the comparison is not between two prices. It is between the membership and a different facility, or none — and per-visit arithmetic answers the wrong question.
- **A month-to-month with no joining fee.** Removing the minimum term removes almost all of the downside, because the cost of being wrong falls to one month. This is worth paying a few dollars a month more for, and is usually the better buy for anyone without a track record.
- **The facility is the commute.** A gym on the route home gets used; one requiring a detour does not. The strongest predictor of realised attendance is distance, and it belongs in the decision ahead of the price.
