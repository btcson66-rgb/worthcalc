## The cost is not the premium

Home warranty pricing is quoted as an annual figure, but almost every contract also charges a **service fee** — a call-out charge — each time a technician visits. That fee is the part most comparisons leave out, and it is charged precisely when you are using the product.

    annual cost = premium + (service fee × visits)

| Visits in a year | Premium | Service fees | Total cost |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | $650 | $0 | $650 |
| 2 | $650 | $200 | **$850** |
| 4 | $650 | $400 | $1,050 |

So the break-even is not $650 of covered repairs. In a two-visit year it is **$850** — and only repairs the contract actually covers, at the amount it actually pays, count toward it.

## The self-insurance comparison

The honest alternative is not "have no plan". It is to pay yourself the same premium.

    value after n years = annual premium × ((1 + rate)^n − 1) ÷ rate

At $650 a year into a savings account paying 4.2%:

| After | Balance |
| --- | ---: |
| 3 years | $2,033 |
| 5 years | $3,535 |
| 10 years | **$7,876** |

Against a typical household repair history, $7,876 covers a great deal. And unlike the warranty, anything you do not spend is still yours, no exclusion can deny it, and you choose the contractor.

## What the warranty actually sells

Framed honestly, a home warranty is not a saving. It sells three things:

1. **A capped worst case.** Your bad year costs the premium plus fees, not $4,500.
2. **A phone number.** Someone else finds the technician. For a landlord or an out-of-town owner this has genuine value.
3. **Budget smoothing.** A predictable monthly figure instead of an unpredictable annual one.

Whether those are worth the margin is a real question with a real answer either way. What they are not is a way to spend less on repairs over time.

## The three exclusions that decide most claims

- **Pre-existing conditions.** A system already failing when the contract starts is generally not covered, and an inspection can be required to establish it.
- **Maintenance and installation.** Failure attributed to lack of servicing or to improper original installation is commonly excluded — a frequent outcome for older HVAC systems.
- **Caps below replacement cost.** A per-item cap of a few thousand dollars against a full HVAC replacement leaves the difference with you, in the single scenario the contract was bought for.

## What would reverse the conclusion

- **No cash buffer.** If a $3,000 repair would go on a credit card at 24%, the capped bill is worth real money and the self-insurance path is not actually available yet.
- **Old systems, known age.** Where an HVAC unit, water heater and two major appliances are all near the end of their service life, expected claims rise toward and past the break-even.
- **A rental property.** The value of a single phone number and a fixed cost per incident is higher when you are not the one meeting the technician.
- **A seller-paid first year.** If the contract comes free with a purchase, the arithmetic for year one is different from the arithmetic for the renewal, and the renewal is where it should be re-run.
