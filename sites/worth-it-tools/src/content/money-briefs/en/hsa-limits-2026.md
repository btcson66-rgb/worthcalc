## The 2026 limits

| | 2026 |
| --- | ---: |
| Individual coverage | **$4,300** |
| Family coverage | **$8,550** |
| Catch-up, age 55+ | +$1,000 |

Eligibility requires enrolment in a qualifying high-deductible health plan and no disqualifying other coverage.

## The part most comparisons miss

An HSA is usually described as "triple tax advantaged": deductible going in, growing untaxed, and untaxed coming out for qualified medical expenses. True, and incomplete.

The fourth advantage is the one with a number on it. A contribution made **through payroll** also escapes the 7.65% payroll tax. A 401(k) deferral does not.

    tax avoided (payroll HSA) = contribution × (income tax rate + payroll tax rate)
    tax avoided (401(k))      = contribution × income tax rate

On a full individual contribution at a 22% marginal rate:

| Route | Rate avoided | Tax avoided on $4,300 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| HSA via payroll | 22% + 7.65% = **29.65%** | **$1,275** |
| HSA contributed directly | 22% | $946 |
| 401(k) deferral | 22% | $946 |

The same $4,300 is worth $329 more through payroll than through any of the alternatives. Contributing directly and deducting it on your return gives up that difference — which is why the enrolment route matters as much as the amount.

## Why it is not really a health account

The rules that make an HSA unusual are the ones about what happens when you *do not* spend it:

- **No deadline.** Unlike an FSA there is no use-it-or-lose-it date. The balance rolls forward indefinitely.
- **It is portable.** It belongs to you, not the employer, and moves with you between jobs.
- **It can be invested.** Most providers allow the balance above a cash threshold into funds.
- **After the qualifying age**, non-medical withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income rather than penalised.

Put together, an unused HSA behaves like a retirement account with better tax treatment on the way in and, for medical spending, on the way out.

## What would reverse the conclusion

- **The underlying plan.** The tax saving is the smaller number in this decision. Compare the premium difference against the difference in worst-case out-of-pocket exposure first; $1,275 of tax relief does not rescue a plan that costs you $4,000 more in a bad year.
- **Cash flow.** Money in an HSA is committed. If contributing the maximum means a medical bill lands on a card at 24%, contribute less and keep the liquidity.
- **Ineligibility you did not notice.** Other coverage — including some forms of secondary or spousal cover — can disqualify you for part or all of a year, and the limit prorates. Publication 969 has the tests.
- **An employer match elsewhere.** A retirement match returns 50–100% immediately and still outranks every rate on this page.
