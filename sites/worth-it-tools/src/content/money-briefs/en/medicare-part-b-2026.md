## The 2026 figures

| | |
| --- | ---: |
| Standard Part B premium | **$202.90/month** |
| Increase from 2025 | **+$17.90** (≈10%) |
| Social Security COLA for the same year | 2.8% |

The premium rose roughly three and a half times faster than the benefit it is usually deducted from. That is the part that gets reported.

## The part that costs more

The late-enrolment penalty is permanent, and most people meet it only once — at which point nothing can be done about it.

    premium with penalty = standard premium × (1 + 0.10 × full 12-month periods delayed)

| Delay | Penalty | Extra per month | Extra over 20 years |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 year | 10% | $20.29 | $4,870 |
| **2 years** | **20%** | **$40.58** | **$9,739** |
| 3 years | 30% | $60.87 | $14,609 |

The penalty is not a fine you pay once. It is added to every monthly premium for as long as you hold Part B, so its total cost scales with how long you live.

## The distinction that decides it

A delay is free or expensive depending on one thing: whether you had **qualifying coverage** during the gap.

| Coverage during the delay | Generally counts? |
| --- | --- |
| Active employer coverage, yours or a spouse's current job | **Yes** — delay normally carries no penalty |
| Retiree coverage | **No** |
| COBRA continuation | **No** |
| Marketplace plan | **No** |

The two lines that catch people are retiree coverage and COBRA. Both feel like insurance, both are insurance, and neither generally stops the penalty clock. Someone who retires at 63, takes 18 months of COBRA and enrols at 66 can arrive at a permanent 10% surcharge having believed they were covered throughout.

## What to do with it

1. **Establish your enrolment window** from your own dates, not from a general rule.
2. **Check what your coverage is**, not what it feels like. Ask the plan administrator in writing whether it is active employer coverage for Medicare purposes.
3. **If it is not qualifying**, enrol on time. The premium is the cheaper of the two numbers on this page.
4. **Budget the net COLA**, not the headline: your benefit × 2.8%, minus your own premium change.

## What would reverse the conclusion

- **Genuine active employer coverage.** A delay under that cover normally carries no penalty at all, and deferring can be the right call while premiums are paid by someone else.
- **An income-related premium.** Higher earners pay above the standard amount, which raises both the premium and the penalty computed on it.
- **A short retirement horizon.** The penalty's total cost is the monthly increment times the years held, so it hurts most for those who hold Part B longest.
