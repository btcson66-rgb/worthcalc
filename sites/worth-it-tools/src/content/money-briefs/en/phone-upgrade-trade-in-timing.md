## What the offer actually is

"$1,000 off with trade-in" almost never means $1,000 off. It means $27.78 a month for 36 months, applied to your bill, for as long as you remain a customer on a qualifying plan.

    realised credit = monthly credit × months you remain

| Months you stay | Credit collected | Forfeited |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 36 | $1,000 | $0 |
| 30 | $833 | $167 |
| 24 | $667 | $333 |
| 18 | $500 | $500 |
| 12 | $333 | $667 |

The offer is not a discount on a phone. It is a discount on staying, priced and advertised as a discount on a phone. Everything else on this page follows from that.

## The full upgrade cost

    effective cost = new device price − realised credit + unpaid balance on the old device

Worked, for an upgrade at month 18 of a previous 24-month agreement:

| Line | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| New device price | $1,100 |
| Trade-in credit, headline | −$1,000 |
| Credit actually collected over 22 months before the next switch | −$611 |
| Unpaid balance on the outgoing device | +$220 |
| **Effective cost** | **$709** |

Against the headline arithmetic of $1,100 − $1,000 = $100, the real number is seven times larger. Neither line that caused the gap is hidden — both are in the agreement — but neither is in the offer.

## The comparison that decides it

Put the carrier offer against an outright sale, valued honestly on both sides:

| | Carrier trade-in | Private sale |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Headline value | $1,000 | $600 |
| Paid | Over 36 months | Immediately |
| Conditional on staying | Yes | No |
| Expected collection over your real tenure | $611 | $600 |
| Tied to a plan tier | Yes | No |
| **Comparable value** | **$611** | **$600** |

At a three-year tenure the carrier offer wins comfortably. At an eighteen-month tenure it loses to a private sale worth 40% less on paper. The deciding variable is not the offer — it is how long you stay, and that is a fact about you rather than about the phone.

## What would reverse the conclusion

- **You genuinely do not switch.** Someone who has been with the same carrier for a decade will collect the full credit, and the offer is then worth close to its headline. The forfeiture risk is real but it is not universal.
- **A credit paid over 24 months rather than 36.** The shorter the run, the more of it you collect, and the closer the offer moves to being what it claims. Comparing two offers on credit length rather than credit size is usually the more informative comparison.
- **The old phone is paid off.** Removing the balance term removes the largest single subtraction, and an upgrade timed to the end of an agreement rather than the middle is materially cheaper for that reason alone.
- **A device that is failing.** If the phone will not hold a charge through a working day or has stopped receiving security updates, the arithmetic above is not the question being asked. Replacing it is a functional decision, and the trade-in structure only decides how to pay for it.
