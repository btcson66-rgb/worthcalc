## What moving actually costs

The mistake is comparing the rent increase to nothing. Moving is not the free option; it is a lump sum that has to be spread over the lease it buys you.

| Line | Typical local move |
| --- | ---: |
| Movers, local, two-bedroom | $900 |
| Security deposit on the new place, net of the old one returned | $600 |
| Application and admin fees | $150 |
| Overlap rent and utilities across the changeover | $450 |
| Utility connection and transfer charges | $200 |
| Time off work and unpaid hours, 24h at $25 | $600 |
| **Total** | **$2,900** |

Two of those lines are the ones usually missing. **Overlap** — almost nobody hands back one set of keys the same hour they collect another. And **time**, which at 24 hours is the second-largest item on the list and the one that never appears on a quote.

## The break-even

    break-even monthly increase = total moving cost ÷ months of the new lease

| New lease length | Break-even increase |
| ---: | ---: |
| 6 months | $483/month |
| 12 months | $242/month |
| 18 months | $161/month |
| 24 months | $121/month |

On a 12-month lease, an increase below **$242 a month** costs less to absorb than to escape. Set that against the rise you were sent:

| Monthly increase | Cost of staying, 12 months | Cost of moving | Cheaper |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| $75 | $900 | $2,900 | **Stay** |
| $150 | $1,800 | $2,900 | **Stay** |
| $242 | $2,900 | $2,900 | Level |
| $350 | $4,200 | $2,900 | **Move** |
| $500 | $6,000 | $2,900 | **Move** |

And note that moving only helps if the new flat is actually cheaper. If it is $150 below your *increased* rent rather than free, the saving is $150 a month, and the break-even test applies to that $150 — not to the increase you were quoted.

## The commute term

A cheaper flat that is further out is not cheaper until the travel is priced:

    real monthly cost = rent + (extra minutes each way × 2 × working days ÷ 60) × value of an hour + extra transport cost

Twenty extra minutes each way, 21 working days, time valued at $20 an hour:

    (20 × 2 × 21 ÷ 60) × $20 = $280 a month

A flat $200 a month cheaper, twenty minutes further out, costs $80 a month more. That term is larger than most rent increases, and it is the reason the cheaper flat is frequently the more expensive decision.

## What would reverse the conclusion

- **A self-move.** Drop the movers and the time off, and the total falls to about $1,400 — a break-even of $117 a month on a 12-month lease. Half the increases that fail the test at $242 pass it at $117.
- **A compounding increase.** A landlord raising 8% every year is not a one-off. Compare the moving cost against the increase over the time you would actually stay, not over one lease, and the decision usually flips inside three years.
- **You were going to move anyway.** If a move is already coming, the marginal cost of doing it now is close to zero and the break-even collapses. The increase then simply sets the date.
- **The new place is genuinely better.** The formula assumes equivalence. A bigger flat, a shorter commute, or a building that fixes things is a quality gain, and the honest comparison prices that before the rent.
