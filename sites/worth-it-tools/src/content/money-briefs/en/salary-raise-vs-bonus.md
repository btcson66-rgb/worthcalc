## Two offers that look identical

"We can do a $3,000 bonus this year, or put $3,000 on your base." Same number, and one of them is worth roughly twenty-seven times the other.

    cumulative value of a raise = raise × ((1 + g)^n − 1) ÷ g

where `g` is the rate of future annual increases and `n` the years you stay.

## The arithmetic

$3,000 added to base, with 3% annual rises thereafter:

| Years | Cumulative extra pay | Bonus equivalent |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | $6,090 | $3,000 |
| 5 | $15,927 | $3,000 |
| 10 | $34,392 | $3,000 |
| **20** | **$80,611** | **$3,000** |

The multiplier at twenty years is about **26.9**. A $3,000 raise is equivalent to a bonus of roughly $80,600 — a number no employer offering the choice is going to put on the table.

## Why the raise keeps growing

A bonus is a payment. A raise is a change to the **rate**, and every subsequent percentage increase is applied on top of it.

The $3,000 does not sit still at $3,000. After one 3% rise it is $3,090; after ten years it is about $4,032 a year and still climbing. The compounding is not investment return — it is the employer's own annual increase applied to a larger base, which is why it happens whether or not you save any of it.

## The second-order effects, all pointing the same way

A raise also lifts everything calculated as a percentage of salary:

- **Employer retirement match**, typically a percentage of pay. A bonus is frequently excluded from the definition of eligible compensation.
- **Pension or profit-sharing contributions**, where they are salary-linked.
- **Future promotions and market adjustments**, which are usually quoted as a percentage of the current base.

None of these attach to a bonus. The table above therefore understates the gap.

## When the bonus is the better answer

- **You expect to leave soon.** Over two years the raise is worth $6,090 against $3,000 — better, but not decisively, and a new employer will price you on market value rather than your old base.
- **The bonus is genuinely much larger.** A $15,000 bonus against a $3,000 raise is a real trade-off at a two-to-three year horizon. Run the factor for your own expected tenure rather than assuming twenty years.
- **You need the cash now.** A raise arrives in twelfths. If the money is for something dated — a deposit, a debt payoff — timing can outrank total value.
- **The raise comes with a title or scope you do not want.** The arithmetic above prices only the money.
