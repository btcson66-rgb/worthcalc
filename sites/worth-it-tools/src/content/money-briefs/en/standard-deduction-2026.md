## The 2026 figures

| Filing status | Standard deduction |
| --- | ---: |
| Married filing jointly | **$32,200** |
| Single / married filing separately | **$16,100** |
| Head of household | **$24,150** |

Those are the numbers itemised deductions have to beat before a single dollar of them changes your tax.

## The arithmetic

    benefit of itemising = (total itemised deductions − standard deduction) × marginal rate

Note what that means: the benefit is **not** the deduction × your rate. It is only the *excess* over what you were getting anyway.

Worked example — a couple filing jointly, first year of a $400,000 mortgage at 6.95%:

| Item | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Mortgage interest, year 1 | **$27,671** |
| Standard deduction, joint | $32,200 |
| **Shortfall on mortgage interest alone** | **−$4,529** |

The largest deduction most households will ever carry does not, by itself, reach the threshold. It needs help from state and local taxes, charitable gifts and qualifying medical expenses before itemising does anything at all.

And when it does clear, only the excess counts. A household reaching $37,600 of total deductions is $5,400 over the line; at a 22% rate that is **$1,188** of actual tax saved — not $8,272, which is what 22% of $37,600 would suggest.

## Why it gets worse every year

Two things move in opposite directions:

- **Mortgage interest falls** as the loan amortises. On the example loan, year-one interest is $27,671; by year ten it is materially lower, because more of each payment is principal.
- **The standard deduction rises** with inflation each year.

A household that itemises in year one and does nothing differently can stop qualifying within a few years. This is a test to re-run annually, not a status you acquire.

## The five-minute version

1. Add up mortgage interest, state and local taxes up to their statutory cap, charitable gifts, and qualifying medical expenses above the applicable floor.
2. Compare that total to your filing status's standard deduction above.
3. Below it → take the standard deduction and stop. The arithmetic is finished.
4. Above it → the benefit is the difference × your marginal rate, not the whole total.

## What would reverse the conclusion

- **A much larger mortgage, or a much higher rate.** Interest scales with both, and a loan large enough clears the threshold on its own.
- **High state and local taxes**, up to whatever cap currently applies. This is the deduction that most often carries a household over the line.
- **A large charitable year.** Bunching two years of giving into one can push a single year above the threshold while the next takes the standard deduction — a legitimate use of timing where the timing is genuinely yours.
- **Significant medical expenses** above the statutory floor, which for a household with a bad health year can be the largest item on the list.
