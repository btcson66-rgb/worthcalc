## The figure

Federal Direct Loans for undergraduates **first disbursed on or after 1 July 2026** carry a fixed **6.52%** for the life of the loan. Loans from earlier years keep their own rate; this one applies to the 2026-27 academic year.

## What the stated rate leaves out

The headline is the rate. The number that surprises people is what the balance does before a single payment is due.

On **unsubsidised** loans, interest accrues from the day each disbursement lands and is capitalised — added to principal — when repayment begins.

    interest accrued ≈ Σ (each year's disbursement × rate × years until repayment)

Worked example — $27,000 borrowed evenly across four years at 6.52%:

| Disbursed | Amount | Years accruing | Interest |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Year 1 | $6,750 | 4 | $1,760 |
| Year 2 | $6,750 | 3 | $1,320 |
| Year 3 | $6,750 | 2 | $880 |
| Year 4 | $6,750 | 1 | $440 |
| **Total** | **$27,000** | | **$4,400** |

The balance entering repayment is not $27,000. It is about **$31,400** — 16% more than was borrowed, before the first payment.

## The ten-year total

    monthly payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n)

| | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Balance at repayment | $31,400 |
| Monthly payment, 10 years at 6.52% | **$356.87** |
| Total repaid | **$42,824** |
| Cost above the $27,000 borrowed | **$15,824** |

Borrow $27,000, repay $42,824. That is 1.59 times the amount borrowed, and roughly $4,400 of it was added before repayment even started.

## The one lever that works during study

Paying the accruing interest while enrolled stops capitalisation. On this example that is **$4,400 of principal you never borrow**, and therefore never pay ten years of interest on.

It is an unusually high-return use of a small income: every dollar paid during study prevents a dollar that would otherwise have compounded at 6.52% for a decade. Few other uses of a student's spare income compete with that.

## Where this sits against other debt

| Rate | Where it ranks |
| --- | --- |
| Credit card, ~24% | Clear first. Nothing competes. |
| **Federal student loan, 6.52%** | **Mid-rate. Ahead of investing at an expected 5-6%, behind an employer match.** |
| Mortgage, ~6.95% | Comparable, but secured and with a tax angle. |

Federal loans also carry protections private debt does not — income-driven plans, deferment, forgiveness programmes — which is a reason to be slower to refinance them away than the rate alone suggests.

## What would reverse the conclusion

- **Subsidised loans.** These do not accrue interest while enrolled at least half-time, so the $4,400 above does not apply to that portion.
- **Origination fees.** Deducted at disbursement, so the amount received is below the amount borrowed and the effective cost is above 6.52%.
- **An income-driven plan or forgiveness programme.** These change the total repaid substantially, in both directions, and a fixed 10-year schedule does not describe them.
- **A shorter repayment term.** The 10-year figure is the standard plan. Paying it in seven cuts the interest materially, at a higher monthly payment.
